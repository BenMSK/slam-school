Week 5 review: continuous-time trajectories

After today you can build a spline or a Gaussian-process trajectory from first principles, work small examples of each by hand, and say what changes when the state is a pose on a Lie group.

About 60 min · No new pages

The week in one paragraph

A continuous-time trajectory replaces "one pose per measurement" with a function of time defined by a few variables, so measurement times, estimation variables, and query times can differ. The parametric version is a spline: a sum of basis functions with local support, so each measurement touches only a few coefficients. Regularizing the coefficients and changing variables turns the spline normal equations into a form where only the kernel matrix \(\mathbf{K}\) appears, and choosing \(\mathbf{K}\) directly gives the nonparametric version, a Gaussian process. If the kernel comes from a white-noise-driven differential equation with a Markovian state, \(\mathbf{K}^{-1}\) is block-tridiagonal and the prior is a chain of binary factors. Conditioning on measurements gives a mean and variance at any time, from the two bracketing states alone. On a Lie group both methods work on differences of neighbouring control poses, mapped to the Lie algebra with Log and back with Exp.

2. Mixed quiz

Check yourself

Q1. A trajectory is a quadratic (degree 2) B-spline. How many coefficients does one measurement at one time stamp involve?

A B-spline of degree d has d + 1 basis functions nonzero at any time: 3 for a quadratic.

Q2. The four active coefficients of a cubic B-spline are all equal to 6. What is the value of the curve at u = 0.37?

The weights are nonnegative and sum to 1 for every u, so a weighted average of four sixes is 6.

Q3. Two measurement times, two basis functions, and Ψ has rows (1, 0) and (1, 1). What is K = ΨΨᵀ?

K₁₁ = 1·1 + 0·0 = 1, K₁₂ = 1·1 + 0·1 = 1, K₂₂ = 1·1 + 1·1 = 2. (ΨᵀΨ, the other order, would give rows (2, 1) and (1, 1).)

Q4. What does the kernel trick replace?

Equation (2.45) needs the basis functions only through K = ΨΨᵀ, so K can be filled in from a kernel function directly.

Q5. Constant-velocity prior on one axis with Q_c = 3 and a step of 1 s. What is the position variance added over the step (the top-left entry of the step covariance)?

Q_c Δt³/3 = 3 · 1/3 = 1. (The velocity variance is Q_c Δt = 3 and the cross term is 1.5.)

Q6. For an SDE prior, K is full but the (1, 4) block of K⁻¹ is zero. What does the zero mean?

Zeros in an information matrix mean no direct factor. The states are still correlated, which is why K has no zeros.

Q7. Random-walk prior with p(0) = 0 and Q_c = 1 (kernel min(t, t′)). One measurement z = 4 at t = 1 with noise variance 1. What is the posterior variance at the query time τ = 3?

k = min(3, 1) = 1 and K + σ² = 2, so the variance is 3 − 1·(1/2)·1 = 2.5. Equivalently: 0.5 at t = 1, plus 1 per second for the 2 s of extrapolation.

Q8. In the linearized random-walk prior factor (2.75), what is the Jacobian of the error with respect to the perturbation of the earlier pose, ξ of k − 1?

e ≈ Log(T_rel) + ξ_k − Ad(T_rel) ξ_{k−1}. The adjoint appears because the earlier pose's perturbation has to be moved through the relative pose.

3. Pen-and-paper problems

  1. A uniform cubic B-spline has knots every \(T = 0.5\) s. On one knot interval the four active coefficients are 2, 4, 4, 0 (oldest first). Find the position and the velocity at \(u = 0.25\).

    Solution

    Weights at \(u = 0.25\): \(w_0 = 0.75^3/6 = 0.0703\), \(w_1 = (3\cdot0.015625 - 6\cdot0.0625 + 4)/6 = 0.6120\), \(w_2 = (-0.046875 + 0.1875 + 0.75 + 1)/6 = 0.3151\), \(w_3 = 0.015625/6 = 0.0026\). They sum to 1.

    Position: \(2\cdot0.0703 + 4\cdot0.6120 + 4\cdot0.3151 + 0 = 3.849\).

    Derivatives of the weights with respect to \(u\): \(-\tfrac12(0.75)^2 = -0.28125\), \(\tfrac12(3\cdot0.0625 - 1) = -0.40625\), \(\tfrac12(-0.1875 + 0.5 + 1) = 0.65625\), \(\tfrac12\cdot0.0625 = 0.03125\). They sum to 0. So \(dp/du = 2\cdot(-0.28125) + 4\cdot(-0.40625) + 4\cdot0.65625 = 0.4375\), and since \(u = t/T\), the velocity is \(0.4375/0.5 = 0.875\) units per second.

  2. A scalar random-walk prior with initial variance 1 and \(Q_c = 1\) is evaluated at \(t = 0\) and \(t = 1\), so \(\mathbf{K} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\). The position is measured at both times with noise variance 1: \(\mathbf{z} = (0, 3)\). Find the estimate (a) from the modified normal equations (2.45) and (b) from the GP regression formula, and check that they agree.

    Solution

    (a) With \(\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{I}\) and \(\mathbf{b} = \mathbf{z}\): \(\mathbf{K}^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\), so \(\mathbf{I} + \mathbf{K}^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\) with determinant 5 and inverse \(\tfrac15\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{bmatrix}\). Then \(\hat{\mathbf{p}} = \tfrac15(2\cdot0 + 1\cdot3,\ 1\cdot0 + 3\cdot3) = (0.6, 1.8)\).

    (b) \(\mathbf{K} + \mathbf{I} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{bmatrix}\), inverse \(\tfrac15\begin{bmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\), so \((\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{I})^{-1}\mathbf{z} = \tfrac15(-3, 6)\) and \(\hat{\mathbf{p}} = \mathbf{K}\cdot\tfrac15(-3,6) = \tfrac15(3, 9) = (0.6, 1.8)\). The same.

    Note how the prior shares information: the estimate at \(t = 0\) is pulled up from 0 to 0.6 by the measurement at \(t = 1\).

  3. Constant-velocity prior on one axis, state \((p, v)\), two times one second apart, \(Q_c = 1\), initial covariance \(\mathbf{I}\). Write the prior as two factors and assemble the \(4\times4\) inverse kernel matrix \(\mathbf{K}^{-1}\). If a third time were added, which block of the new \(6\times6\) matrix would be zero?

    Solution

    Factors: \(\norm{\mathbf{x}_1}^2_{\mathbf{I}} + \norm{\mathbf{x}_2 - \boldsymbol{\Phi}\mathbf{x}_1}^2_{\mathbf{Q}_2}\) with \(\boldsymbol{\Phi} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\) and \(\mathbf{Q}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1/3 & 1/2 \\ 1/2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\). Its determinant is \(1/12\), so \(\mathbf{Q}_2^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 12 & -6 \\ -6 & 4 \end{bmatrix}\).

    Expanding the second factor, the blocks of \(\mathbf{K}^{-1}\) are: bottom-right \(\mathbf{Q}_2^{-1}\); top-right \(-\boldsymbol{\Phi}\T\mathbf{Q}_2^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} -12 & 6 \\ -6 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\); top-left \(\mathbf{I} + \boldsymbol{\Phi}\T\mathbf{Q}_2^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Phi} = \begin{bmatrix} 13 & 6 \\ 6 & 5 \end{bmatrix}\). So

    \[ \mathbf{K}^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 13 & 6 & -12 & 6 \\ 6 & 5 & -6 & 2 \\ -12 & -6 & 12 & -6 \\ 6 & 2 & -6 & 4 \end{bmatrix} . \]

    With a third time the only new factor links states 2 and 3, so the block between states 1 and 3 (and its transpose) is zero. You can check the first two rows and the zero block in the Day 3 widget with "constant velocity" and three times.

  4. On \(\SE(2)\), \(\Exp\big((\rho_1,\rho_2,\theta)\big)\) has rotation by \(\theta\) and translation \(\mathbf{V}(\theta)\,(\rho_1,\rho_2)\) with \(\mathbf{V}(\theta) = \tfrac{1}{\theta}\begin{bmatrix} \sin\theta & -(1-\cos\theta) \\ 1-\cos\theta & \sin\theta \end{bmatrix}\). Let \(\mathbf{T}_{k-1} = \mathbf{I}\) and let \(\mathbf{T}_k\) have rotation 90° and translation \((1, 1)\). (a) Find \(\boldsymbol{\xi} = \Log(\mathbf{T}_k\mathbf{T}_{k-1}^{-1})\). (b) Find the interpolated pose at \(\alpha = 0.5\) and its distance from the midpoint of the straight line. (c) A random-walk prior has \(T\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Q}} = \diag(0.1, 0.1, 0.05)\). What does this factor contribute to the cost, \(\norm{\mathbf{e}_k}^2_{T\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Q}}}\)?

    Solution

    (a) \(\theta = \pi/2\), so \(\mathbf{V} = \tfrac{2}{\pi}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\) and \(\mathbf{V}^{-1} = \tfrac{\pi}{4}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\). Then \((\rho_1,\rho_2) = \mathbf{V}^{-1}(1,1) = (\pi/2, 0)\) and \(\boldsymbol{\xi} = (\pi/2,\ 0,\ \pi/2) \approx (1.571, 0, 1.571)\). This is "drive forward while turning at the same rate": a quarter circle of radius 1.

    (b) \(\alpha\boldsymbol{\xi} = (\pi/4, 0, \pi/4)\). The rotation is 45°. The translation is \(\mathbf{V}(\pi/4)\,(\pi/4, 0) = (\sin 45^\circ,\ 1 - \cos 45^\circ) = (0.707, 0.293)\). That point lies on the circle of radius 1 centred at \((0,1)\), as it should. The straight-line midpoint is \((0.5, 0.5)\), at distance \(\sqrt{0.207^2 + 0.207^2} = 0.293\).

    (c) \(\mathbf{e}_k = \boldsymbol{\xi}\), so the cost is \((\pi/2)^2/0.1 + 0 + (\pi/2)^2/0.05 = 24.7 + 49.3 = 74.0\). A prior this tight strongly resists a quarter turn in one segment; measurements would have to pay for it.
