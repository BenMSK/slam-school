Week 17 · Review

Week 17 review: what a visual-inertial system can and cannot know

After today you can build an observability matrix, list the unobservable directions of an aided inertial system for a given motion and sensor, and explain how a sliding-window estimator keeps them unobservable.

About 60 min · No new pages

The week in one paragraph

An IMU aided by a camera or LiDAR estimates orientation, velocity, position, two biases, and landmarks. Linearize it: the error state evolves through a transition matrix \(\boldsymbol{\Phi}\), and each measurement has a Jacobian that sees features only relative to the sensor. Stacking \(\mathbf{H}_{x_k}\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{(k,1)}\) gives the observability matrix \(\mathbf{M}\), whose nullspace always contains four directions: three shifts and a rotation about gravity. These are a choice of world frame. Some motions add directions that are not: without rotation, tilt hides in the accelerometer bias; with constant acceleration and a monocular camera, scale hides there too; without parallax, feature depth is lost. A practical VIO optimizes a sliding window, marginalizes old states, and uses first-estimate Jacobians so that marginalization does not create information about yaw.

2. Mixed quiz

Check yourself

Q1. The cart of Day 1 has state \((p, v, b)\) and a position sensor. What is the smallest number of readings that makes it fully observable?

Each reading adds one row to \(\mathbf{M}\). Three unknowns need rank 3, and the rows \([1, k, -k^2/2]\) for \(k = 0, 1, 2\) are independent.

Q2. In \(\boldsymbol{\Phi}\) of (11.41), the column of the position error holds a single identity block, in the position row. What does that say?

Nothing in the IMU kinematics depends on where the robot is. An initial position error stays the same size and feeds nothing else.

Q3. A point is at \(\hat{\mathbf{p}}^c_f = (2, 0, 4)\T\). For a pinhole bearing \((x/z, y/z)\), what is the first row of \(\mathbf{H}_b\)?

\(\tfrac1z[1,\ 0,\ -x/z] = \tfrac14[1,\ 0,\ -0.5] = [0.25,\ 0,\ -0.125]\). Check: times \((2, 0, 4)\) gives \(0.5 - 0.5 = 0\).

Q4. Which part of a line's Plücker coordinates determines its image?

\(\boldsymbol{\ell} = \mathbf{K}\mathbf{n}^c_\ell\). The moment is the normal of the plane through the camera centre and the line, and the image line is where that plane meets the image.

Q5. A stereo camera and an IMU move with generic motion among unknown landmarks. How many unobservable directions are there?

Global position (3) and yaw (1). A stereo camera measures range as well as bearing, but that does not tie the system to any global frame.

Q6. A drone with a monocular camera and an IMU flies in a straight line with constant acceleration and does not rotate. Which extra quantities become unobservable?

No rotation is row 1 of Table 11.1 (global orientation). Constant acceleration with a monocular camera is row 2 (system scale).

Q7. Scalar states \(x_0, x_1\): a prior on \(x_0\) with information 4, and an odometry factor with information 4. What is the information on \(x_1\) after marginalizing \(x_0\)?

\(\boldsymbol{\Lambda} = \begin{bmatrix} 8 & -4 \\ -4 & 4 \end{bmatrix}\), so \(4 - (-4)\cdot\tfrac18\cdot(-4) = 2\). Variance 0.5 = 0.25 + 0.25.

Q8. What do first-estimate Jacobians hold fixed?

The estimates keep changing. Only the point where their Jacobians are evaluated is kept, so that the prior and the newer factors share the same yaw null vector.

The lab again, for self-testing. Before you change a setting, say how many eigenvalues will be zero and which changes will be invisible. Then check.

Notice: every answer follows from three questions. Does the body rotate (if not, tilt joins the list)? Does the body-frame acceleration change (if not, and the camera is bearing-only, scale joins the list)? Does the body translate at all (if not, and the camera is bearing-only, the depth of each landmark joins the list)?

3. Pen-and-paper problems

  1. The cart of Day 1 has state \((p, v, b)\), \(\boldsymbol{\Phi}^k = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & k & -k^2/2 \\ 0 & 1 & -k \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\), and a velocity sensor \(\mathbf{H} = [0\ 1\ 0]\). (a) Write \(\mathbf{M}\) for three readings and find its rank and nullspace. (b) Show that one extra position reading makes the system observable.

    Solution

    (a) The rows are \(\mathbf{H}\boldsymbol{\Phi}^k = [0, 1, -k]\) for \(k = 0, 1, 2\):

    \[ \mathbf{M} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & -2 \end{bmatrix} . \]

    Row 3 equals \(2\times\) row 2 minus row 1, and rows 1 and 2 are independent, so the rank is 2. The first column is zero, so the nullspace is spanned by \((1, 0, 0)\): the initial position.

    (b) A position reading at step \(k\) adds the row \([1, k, -k^2/2]\). Its product with \((1, 0, 0)\) is 1, not 0, so that vector is no longer in the nullspace. The nullspace had dimension 1, so it is now \(\{\mathbf{0}\}\) and the rank is 3.

  2. A point is at \(\hat{\mathbf{p}}^c_f = (2, -1, 2)\T\) m. (a) Compute \(\mathbf{H}_r\) and the pinhole \(\mathbf{H}_b\). (b) Verify \(\mathbf{H}_b\hat{\mathbf{p}}^c_f = \mathbf{0}\). (c) The point moves by 3 cm along the sensor's \(x\) axis. By how much do the range and the bearing change?

    Solution

    (a) \(\norm{\mathbf{p}} = \sqrt{4 + 1 + 4} = 3\), so \(\mathbf{H}_r = [\tfrac23,\ -\tfrac13,\ \tfrac23]\). With \(z = 2\), \(x/z = 1\), \(y/z = -0.5\):

    \[ \mathbf{H}_b = \tfrac12\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0 & -0.5 \\ 0 & 0.5 & 0.25 \end{bmatrix} . \]

    (b) First row: \(0.5\cdot 2 - 0.5\cdot 2 = 0\). Second row: \(0.5\cdot(-1) + 0.25\cdot 2 = 0\).

    (c) \(\delta\mathbf{p} = (0.03, 0, 0)\). Range: \(\tfrac23 \times 0.03 = 0.02\) m. Bearing: \((0.5 \times 0.03,\ 0) = (0.015,\ 0)\) in normalized image coordinates.

  3. Work in a horizontal plane, so gravity does not enter. A robot does not rotate (\(\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{I}\)), starts at the origin with velocity \((1, 0)\) m/s, and has constant acceleration \((0.2, 0)\) m/s². Its accelerometer bias is \((0.05, 0)\) m/s². A landmark is at \((4, 2)\) m and the camera measures bearing only. (a) Scale the solution by \(s = 2\). Give the new velocity, landmark, and the bias that keeps the accelerometer reading unchanged. (b) Check that the bearing at \(t = 2\) s is the same in both solutions. (c) Now the acceleration is 0.2 m/s² during the first second and 0.6 m/s² afterwards. Show that no \(s \ne 1\) fits.

    Solution

    (a) Velocity \((2, 0)\), acceleration \((0.4, 0)\), landmark \((8, 4)\). The reading is \(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} = (0.25, 0)\). The scaled solution needs \(0.4 + b'_x = 0.25\), so \(\mathbf{b}' = (-0.15, 0)\). This is \(\mathbf{b}' = \mathbf{b} - (s-1)\mathbf{a}\).

    (b) Original: \(x(2) = 1\cdot 2 + \tfrac12\cdot 0.2\cdot 4 = 2.4\), so the bearing is \(\operatorname{atan2}(2,\ 4 - 2.4) = \operatorname{atan2}(2, 1.6) \approx 51.3^\circ\). Scaled: \(x(2) = 4.8\), bearing \(\operatorname{atan2}(4,\ 8 - 4.8) = \operatorname{atan2}(4, 3.2) \approx 51.3^\circ\).

    (c) The readings are \(0.25\) and then \(0.65\). A scaled solution needs \(0.2s + b'_x = 0.25\) and \(0.6s + b'_x = 0.65\) with one constant \(b'_x\). Subtracting gives \(0.4s = 0.4\), so \(s = 1\). The change in acceleration fixes the scale; the bias can only absorb a constant.

  4. Three scalar positions \(x_0, x_1, x_2\) are linked by two odometry factors, \(x_1 - x_0 = u_1\) and \(x_2 - x_1 = u_2\), each with information 1. There is no prior. (a) Write the \(3\times 3\) information matrix and show that \((1, 1, 1)\) is a null vector. (b) Marginalize \(x_0\) and show that the result still has the null vector \((1, 1)\). (c) Suppose an inconsistent relinearization adds 0.1 to the \((x_1, x_1)\) entry of the result. What variance does the estimator now report for \(x_2\), and what should it be?

    Solution

    (a) Each factor contributes \(\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\) on its two variables:

    \[ \boldsymbol{\Lambda} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 2 & -1 \\ 0 & -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \boldsymbol{\Lambda}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} . \]

    Shifting all three positions together changes no measurement.

    (b) \(\Lambda_{mm} = 1\), \(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{rm} = (-1, 0)\T\), \(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{rr} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\). The Schur complement subtracts \(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{rm}\Lambda_{mm}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{mr} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}\):

    \[ \boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\text{prior}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\text{prior}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{0} . \]

    (c) The matrix becomes \(\begin{bmatrix} 1.1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\) with determinant \(1.1 - 1 = 0.1\). It is now invertible, with inverse \(\tfrac{1}{0.1}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1.1 \end{bmatrix}\), so the reported variance of \(x_2\) is 11. The correct answer is "infinite": no measurement says where the chain sits. A small inconsistency has turned an unobservable direction into a confident estimate. This is the one-dimensional picture of what first-estimate Jacobians prevent.
