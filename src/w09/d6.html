Week 9 · Review

Week 9 review: from local minima to certified optima

After today you can lift a QCQP and bound it with Shor's relaxation, reduce pose-graph optimization to its rotation-only form, test a solution with the certificate matrix, and describe how SE-Sync solves the relaxation.

About 60 min · No new pages

The week in one paragraph

SLAM costs are non-convex, so a local solver can stop at a local minimum without knowing it. For pose-graph optimization there is a way to know. With Langevin rotation noise and Gaussian translation noise the maximum-likelihood cost is quadratic; the translations can be eliminated, leaving \(\min\tr(\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{R}\T\mathbf{R})\) over rotations. Lifting \(\mathbf{R}\T\mathbf{R}\) to a positive semidefinite matrix \(\mathbf{Z}\) and dropping its rank constraint gives a convex semidefinite program whose optimum is a lower bound, and for small noise is exact. SE-Sync solves that program through a low-rank factor \(\mathbf{Y}\) on Stiefel manifolds, raising the rank until the solution is rank-deficient, then rounds to rotations and compares costs. Equal costs are a proof of global optimality.

2. Mixed quiz

Check yourself

Q1. A clean ring of 8 headings (κ = 1, no noise) is solved from a random start and the solver stops at the once-twisted configuration. What cost does it report?

Each of the 8 edges has residual 45°, so the cost is 4 × 8 × (1 − cos 45°) = 32 × 0.293 = 9.37. Since 1 is less than 8/4, it is a local minimum.

Q2. Which statement holds for every relaxation of a minimization problem, tight or not?

A relaxation minimizes the same objective over a larger set, so its optimum cannot be higher. The other three properties hold only in special cases.

Q3. For M = [[1, 2], [2, 0]] and x = (1, 1), what is tr(M xxᵀ)?

tr(M xxᵀ) = xᵀMx = 1·1 + 2·(2·1·1) + 0·1 = 5. Directly: xxᵀ is the all-ones matrix, and the entries of M sum to 5.

Q4. Shor's relaxation of a QCQP returns X* with eigenvalues 2, 1, 0 and value d* = 7. What follows?

Two nonzero eigenvalues mean rank 2, so X* is not a lifted point. The value 7 is still a valid lower bound on p*.

Q5. A pose graph consists of two separate pieces with no measurement between them. How many zero eigenvalues does its Laplacian have?

The number of zero eigenvalues equals the number of connected components (Box 6.1). The book assumes a connected graph, which has exactly one.

Q6. After the translations are eliminated from a 3-D pose graph with n poses, what are the unknowns of Problem 6.2?

The translations are functions of the rotations through (6.16), so only R = (R_1 … R_n) remains. The 3n × 3n matrix Z appears only after the lift.

Q7. At the output R̂ of a local solver, the certificate matrix has eigenvalues 0, 0, 0.4, 0.4, 1.3, 1.3 (planar, three poses). What do you conclude?

No eigenvalue is negative, so S ⪰ 0 and R̂ is certified. The d = 2 zeros are always there, because S R̂ᵀ = 0.

Q8. For d = 3 the staircase is at r = 5 and returns a second-order critical point Y* with singular values 9.1, 9.1, 9.1, 0, 0. What is the situation?

Rank 3 is below r = 5, so Theorem 6.2 applies. Rank exactly d is the signature of an exact relaxation, equation (6.25).

3. Pen-and-paper problems

  1. Let \(\mathbf{x}=(1,-2)\) and \(\mathbf{C}=\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2\\ 2 & -1\end{bmatrix}\). (a) Compute \(\mathbf{x}\T\mathbf{C}\mathbf{x}\). (b) Form \(\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{x}\mathbf{x}\T\) and compute \(\tr(\mathbf{C}\mathbf{X})\). (c) Find the eigenvalues of \(\mathbf{X}\) and recover \(\mathbf{x}\) from them.

    Solution

    (a) \(1\cdot1+2\cdot2\cdot(1)(-2)+(-1)\cdot4=1-8-4=-11\).

    (b) \(\mathbf{X}=\begin{bmatrix}1 & -2\\ -2 & 4\end{bmatrix}\). \(\tr(\mathbf{C}\mathbf{X})=\sum_{i,j}C_{ij}X_{ij}=1\cdot1+2\cdot(-2)+2\cdot(-2)+(-1)\cdot4=-11\). The two agree, as (6.1) says.

    (c) The trace is 5 and the determinant is \(4-4=0\), so the eigenvalues are 5 and 0: rank 1. The eigenvector for 5 is \(\mathbf{u}=(1,-2)/\sqrt5\), and \(\sqrt5\,\mathbf{u}=(1,-2)\). The other sign, \((-1,2)\), gives the same \(\mathbf{X}\).

  2. Minimize \(\mathbf{x}\T\mathbf{C}\mathbf{x}\) over unit vectors \(\mathbf{x}\in\R^2\) with \(\mathbf{C}=\begin{bmatrix}2 & 1\\ 1 & 2\end{bmatrix}\). (a) Solve Shor's relaxation using the disk coordinates \(u=X_{11}-X_{22}\), \(v=2X_{12}\) of Day 2. (b) Is it exact? Give \(\mathbf{x}^\star\). (c) Write the dual and verify the certificate.

    Solution

    (a) \(\tr(\mathbf{C}\mathbf{X})=\tfrac12(C_{11}+C_{22})+\tfrac12(C_{11}-C_{22})u+C_{12}v=2+v\) on the disk \(u^2+v^2\le1\). The minimum is at \((u,v)=(0,-1)\) with \(d^\star=1\).

    (b) That point is on the boundary, so the rank is 1. \(X_{11}=X_{22}=\tfrac12\), \(X_{12}=-\tfrac12\), which is \(\mathbf{x}^\star\mathbf{x}^{\star\mathsf{T}}\) for \(\mathbf{x}^\star=(1,-1)/\sqrt2\). Check: \(\mathbf{x}^{\star\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{x}^\star=\tfrac12(2-2+2)=1\). Exact, \(p^\star=d^\star=1\).

    (c) Dual: maximize \(\lambda\) subject to \(\mathbf{C}-\lambda\mathbf{I}\succeq0\). The eigenvalues of \(\mathbf{C}\) are 1 and 3, so \(\lambda=1\) and \(\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{C}-\mathbf{I}=\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1\\ 1 & 1\end{bmatrix}\), with eigenvalues 0 and 2: positive semidefinite. And \(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x}^\star=\mathbf{0}\). Both certificate conditions hold, and \(\mathbf{b}\T\boldsymbol{\lambda}=1\) equals the cost.

  3. One planar edge \(i\to j\) has \(\kappa=8\), \(\tau=4\), and measurement \(\tilde\theta_{ij}=60^\circ\), \(\tilde{\mathbf{t}}_{ij}=(1,0.5)\). The current estimate is \(\theta_i=0\), \(\mathbf{t}_i=(0,0)\), \(\theta_j=90^\circ\), \(\mathbf{t}_j=(1,1)\). (a) What does this edge contribute to the cost (6.9)? (b) Which \(\kappa\) corresponds to an angular standard deviation of 5°?

    Solution

    (a) Rotation: the residual is \(r=90^\circ-0^\circ-60^\circ=30^\circ\), so the term is \(4\kappa(1-\cos30^\circ)=32\times0.134=4.29\). Translation: \(\mathbf{R}_i=\mathbf{I}\), so the residual is \((1,1)-(0,0)-(1,0.5)=(0,0.5)\), with squared norm 0.25, and the term is \(4\times0.25=1\). Total: 5.29.

    (b) For small noise the angle is nearly Gaussian with variance \(1/(2\kappa)\). \(5^\circ=0.0873\) rad, so \(\kappa=1/(2\times0.0873^2)=1/0.01523\approx66\).

  4. A clean ring of \(n=8\) planar headings (\(\kappa=1\), perfect measurements) and the once-twisted critical point \(\hat{\mathbf{R}}\) with residual 45° on every edge. (a) Compute its cost and its multipliers \(\lambda_i\), and check that \(\tr(\boldsymbol{\Lambda})\) equals the cost. (b) Without computing any eigenvalue, show that \(\mathbf{S}=\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}-\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\) is not positive semidefinite. (c) What general fact does (b) prove?

    Solution

    (a) Cost: \(4\times8\times(1-\cos45^\circ)=32\times0.2929=9.37\). Each pose has two edges, so \(\lambda_i=2(1-\cos45^\circ)=0.586\) and \(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_i=0.586\,\mathbf{I}_2\). Then \(\tr(\boldsymbol{\Lambda})=8\times2\times0.586=9.37\), the cost.

    (b) Let \(\bar{\mathbf{R}}\) hold the true rotations. They fit the perfect measurements, so \(\tr(\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\T\bar{\mathbf{R}})=0\). The diagonal blocks of \(\bar{\mathbf{R}}\T\bar{\mathbf{R}}\) are identities, so \(\tr(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\T\bar{\mathbf{R}})=\tr(\boldsymbol{\Lambda})=9.37\). Therefore

    \[ \tr\bigl(\bar{\mathbf{R}}\mathbf{S}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\T\bigr)=\tr(\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\T\bar{\mathbf{R}})-\tr(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\T\bar{\mathbf{R}})=0-9.37\lt0 . \]

    The left side is the sum of \(\mathbf{v}\T\mathbf{S}\mathbf{v}\) over the two rows \(\mathbf{v}\) of \(\bar{\mathbf{R}}\). A negative sum needs a negative term, so some \(\mathbf{v}\) has \(\mathbf{v}\T\mathbf{S}\mathbf{v}\lt0\).

    (c) The same computation with any better feasible \(\mathbf{R}\) gives \(\tr(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{R}\T)=f(\mathbf{R})-f(\hat{\mathbf{R}})\lt0\). So a critical point that is not globally optimal can never pass the test \(\mathbf{S}\succeq0\): the certificate has no false positives.
