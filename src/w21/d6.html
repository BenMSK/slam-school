Week 21 review: SLAM in a world that changes

After today you can classify a changing scene, write the factors that track a moving object, clear and compare maps with free-space evidence, update an object's persistence, and say what makes deformable SLAM solvable.

About 60 min · No new pages

The week in one paragraph

Chapter 15 drops the static-world assumption. Motion in view is short-term: it can be rejected as outliers, tracked as rigid bodies in the factor graph, or reconstructed densely. Change out of view is long-term: it needs re-identification, maps that record free space so that "gone" differs from "not looked at", and per-object persistence estimates. When nothing is static, one camera leaves the problem under-constrained (\(3NF\) unknowns, \(2NF\) equations), and deformation priors make it solvable.

2. Mixed quiz

Check yourself

Q1. What decides whether a robot experiences a change as short-term or long-term?

Fast motion sampled fast enough is short-term; a small rearrangement while the robot looked away is long-term.

Q2. A 5 Hz radar watches drones flying at 20 m/s, 2 m apart. Can nearest-neighbour matching between scans be trusted?

d = 20 / 5 = 4 m, so the nearest detection is usually another drone.

Q3. A visual SLAM system masks every pixel labelled "car". What goes wrong in a full car park?

Labels mark potentially dynamic classes; combine them with geometric checks.

Q4. Which variables does the coupling factor (15.3) connect?

It compares the point under the pose at i+1 with the point under the pose at i advanced by (15.4). No camera appears.

Q5. A long-free grid cell sits at the lower clamp, log-odds −2. A hit adds 0.7. How many hits make it solid (above 0)?

−2 → −1.3 → −0.6 → +0.1: three hits.

Q6. After a second visit the map shows half a table floating in the room. What happened, and what is the usual remedy?

Semantic consistency: scenes change in units, the robot sees parts.

Q7. During a visit, a mapped object is outside the camera's field of view. With no forgetting, how does its persistence probability change?

No observation, no update: absence of evidence.

Q8. Why is deformable SLAM much easier with a depth camera than with a monocular one?

3NF equations for 3NF unknowns; the priors no longer have to supply the depth.

3. Pen-and-paper problems

  1. A security robot has a 10 Hz LiDAR. People walk at 1.2 m/s and stay at least 0.8 m apart. (a) How far does a person move between scans, as a fraction of the spacing? (b) The robot passes each corridor once every 5 minutes. How far can a person move between passes?

    Solution

    (a) \(d = 1.2/10 = 0.12\) m, 15% of the spacing: short-term, track from scan to scan. (b) \(1.2 \times 300 = 360\) m: long-term, nothing can be tracked across the gap.

  2. A planar object is at position \((2, 1)\) m with heading 0. In its own frame \(\mathbf{v} = (1.5, 0)\) m/s and \(\omega = 1\) rad/s; \(\Delta t = 0.1\) s. (a) Give the increment (15.4) and the predicted pose. (b) Where does the model put the object point \((0, 1)\)? (c) The estimate of the next pose is position \((2.20, 1.00)\), heading 0.1 rad. Evaluate (15.3) for that point.

    Solution

    (a) Rotation by 0.1 rad, translation \((0.15, 0)\). The heading is 0, so the predicted pose is position \((2.15, 1)\), heading 0.1 rad. (b) \((0,1)\) rotated by 0.1 rad is \((-\sin 0.1, \cos 0.1) = (-0.100, 0.995)\); adding the position gives \((2.050, 1.995)\). (c) The estimate has the same heading, so it puts the point at \((2.100, 1.995)\). The residual is \((0.050, 0)\) m.

  3. An occupancy grid uses \(l_{\text{hit}} = 0.85\), \(l_{\text{miss}} = 0.40\), and clamps log-odds to \([-2, 3.5]\). A forklift parks on a long-free cell, is hit in 10 consecutive scans, and drives away. (a) What is the cell's log-odds when it leaves? (b) How many scans until the cell is free again? (c) How many without the upper clamp?

    Solution

    (a) \(-2 + 10 \times 0.85 = 6.5\), clamped to 3.5 (reached at the seventh hit). (b) \(3.5 - 0.40\,n \le 0\) needs \(n \ge 8.75\): 9 scans. (c) \(6.5/0.40 = 16.25\): 17 scans. The clamp bounds how long the map takes to accept a change.

  4. (Stretch.) A fixed camera tracks \(N = 100\) points on a deforming surface through \(F = 50\) frames. (a) Count unknowns and equations with no prior. (b) Assume every frame's shape is a weighted sum of \(K = 3\) basis shapes shared by all frames, \(\mathbf{S}_f = \sum_{k} c_{f,k} \mathbf{B}_k\). Count again. (c) What is the largest \(K\) with no more unknowns than equations, and why is a larger \(K\) not simply better?

    Solution

    (a) \(3NF = 15000\) unknowns, \(2NF = 10000\) equations. (b) \(3NK + KF = 900 + 150 = 1050\) unknowns for the same 10000 equations. (c) \(3NK + KF \le 2NF\) gives \(K \le 10000/350 = 28.6\), so \(K = 28\). The basis is the prior: few basis shapes allow only a small family of deformations, which is what fixes the depths. At \(K = F\) every frame can have its own shape and the prior says nothing. This is the low-dimensional basis of early NRSfM (p. 448).
