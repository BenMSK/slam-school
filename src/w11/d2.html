Today's big idea

A camera records directions to things, not distances. Depth appears only when the same point is seen from two places, and its accuracy depends on how far apart those places are. Everything in visual SLAM, from the 19th-century surveyors to a headset tracking in real time, is a way of organizing that triangulation over many points and many views.

1. An old problem

Reconstructing the world from pictures is almost as old as photography. The mathematics the back-end runs today was written down before computers existed. §7.1.1 · pp. 193–194

The step from offline reconstruction to SLAM needed causal methods: methods that estimate using only the images seen so far, not the whole collection. §7.1.1 · p. 194

2. Five overlapping terms

The book notes that these words are often used interchangeably. They describe similar computations with different emphasis. §7.1.2 · pp. 194–195

TermWhat it isWhat sets it apart
PhotogrammetryThe science of taking measurements and 3D reconstructions from overlapping photographsThe oldest and broadest term; camera positions are often assumed known
Bundle adjustment (BA)An optimization: adjust camera poses (optionally intrinsics) and 3D points to minimize reprojection errorIt is a solver step, not a system. It needs a good initial guess and outlier rejection
Structure from motion (SfM)Estimate the 3D structure and the camera motion together from a collection of imagesUsually offline and non-causal; images may be unordered; ends with BA
Visual odometry (VO)Estimate camera motion from consecutive video framesLocal: a sliding window of recent frames, no global map, so it drifts
Visual SLAMLocalize and map in real time in an unknown environmentAdds place recognition, relocalization, and loop closure to VO

The reprojection error in the BA row is the distance in the image between where a point was observed and where the current 3D model says it should appear. You will write it as a formula on Day 5. BA minimizes it with the Gauss-Newton or Levenberg-Marquardt methods of Week 2 · Day 4 and Week 2 · Day 5.

3. Why vision is harder than LiDAR: depth is not measured

A LiDAR returns the distance to each point. A camera returns the projection of the scene's light onto its sensor, and the 3D geometry has to be inferred. That is why the book calls the visual problem more challenging. §7.2 · p. 196

Filling the gap: depth from two views, and how uncertain it is

Take the simplest case, seen from above. Two identical cameras look along the \(z\) axis. Camera 2 sits a distance \(b\) (the baseline) to the right of camera 1. A point at sideways position \(x\) and depth \(z\) (in camera 1's frame) appears at image columns

\[ u_1 = f\,\frac{x}{z}, \qquad u_2 = f\,\frac{x-b}{z}, \]

where \(f\) is the focal length in pixels and columns are counted from the image center (tomorrow's lesson derives this). The unknown \(x\) cancels in the difference, called the disparity:

\[ d = u_1 - u_2 = \frac{f\,b}{z} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad z = \frac{f\,b}{d}. \]

Far points have small disparity. Now let the disparity be wrong by a small amount \(\delta d\). Differentiate:

\[ \frac{\partial z}{\partial d} = -\frac{f\,b}{d^2} = -\frac{z^2}{f\,b} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sigma_z \approx \frac{z^2}{f\,b}\,\sigma_d . \]

If each column is measured with independent noise of standard deviation \(\sigma\), then \(\sigma_d = \sqrt{2}\,\sigma\). Numbers: \(f = 500\) px, \(b = 0.5\) m, \(z = 20\) m gives \(d = 12.5\) px. With \(\sigma = 1\) px, \(\sigma_z \approx \frac{400}{250}\times 1.41 = 2.26\) m, an 11% error from one pixel of noise.

Two consequences shape every visual SLAM system. Depth error grows with the square of depth. And it shrinks in proportion to the baseline, so a camera must move sideways enough before a new point can be placed.

Try it: double the baseline, then double the depth.

Notice that the cloud of triangulated points is a thin needle along the viewing direction: sideways position is known well, depth poorly. At the defaults the predicted depth σ is 2.26 m. A baseline of 1 m halves it; a depth of 10 m divides it by four. At the far-left baseline with depth 30 m the disparity is under 2 px, the cloud smears over tens of meters, and some samples give no usable depth at all (the count of valid samples drops below 300).

A stereo rig has a fixed, known baseline. A single moving camera has to use its own motion as the baseline, and that motion is itself unknown. This is the root of two issues you will meet again: a monocular system needs a special initialization step (Day 5), and it cannot recover the absolute scale of the scene.

4. The three parts of a visual SLAM system

Like every SLAM system since Week 1 · Day 1, a visual one is split into stages. The book names three sub-functions that complement each other. §7.2 · p. 196

  • The visual odometry front-end estimates the relative motion between consecutive frames and so provides the initial estimate of the camera pose. §7.2.1 · p. 196
  • The mapping back-end refines trajectory and map with global optimization, BA or pose-graph optimization, and folds in the constraints found by place recognition. The result is long-term consistency. §7.2.2 · pp. 196–197
  • Visual place recognition and relocalization matches the current image against earlier ones, with classical descriptors or trained networks. It detects revisits, which corrects drift, and it finds the camera again when tracking is lost. §7.2.3 · p. 197

These run as parallel threads. Heavy jobs like global optimization are scheduled so that tracking stays real-time. §7.2.4 · p. 197

5. Two ways to do visual odometry

AspectFeature-based (indirect)Direct
StepsDetect feature points, match them across images, then estimate motionOne step: optimize over the images themselves
MinimizesReprojection error, over camera motion and 3D pointsA photometric loss (differences of pixel brightness), over camera motion and 3D structure
Data associationExplicit: decided by the matching stepImplicit: decided by the optimization
Closest relativeClassical BAOptical flow; "photometric BA"
Basin of convergenceLargerSmaller, at least in the basic formulation

The basin of convergence is the set of starting guesses from which the optimizer reaches the right answer. Matched features can pull a poor initial pose a long way, because the match itself says where the point went. Raw brightness only gives a useful gradient within a pixel or two of the right place. Direct methods therefore work coarse-to-fine: align heavily down-sampled images first, where a large motion is a small number of pixels, then refine at higher resolution. Some align learned feature maps instead of brightness. §7.2.1 · p. 196 Week 12 treats both alignments in detail.

Common mistakes

  • "Bundle adjustment is a kind of SLAM system." BA is the optimization problem at the core. SfM and visual SLAM are systems that call it.
  • "Visual odometry plus a map is visual SLAM." The defining addition is recognizing previously visited places and correcting the trajectory around the loop.
  • "More pixels of resolution fix depth accuracy." Depth error scales with \(z^2/(f\,b)\). Without baseline there is no depth, however sharp the image.

Check yourself

Q1. A stereo camera has f = 600 px and baseline 0.12 m. A point has disparity 9 px. How far away is it?

z = f·b/d = 600 × 0.12 / 9 = 8 m.

Q2. With the same rig and the same pixel noise, a point moves from 5 m to 10 m away. Its depth standard deviation is multiplied by about

σ_z ≈ z²·σ_d/(f·b), so doubling z quadruples the depth uncertainty.

Q3. Someone downloads 5,000 unordered tourist photos of a cathedral and computes camera poses and a 3D model overnight. Which term fits best?

Offline, non-causal, unordered images, joint estimation of structure and camera motion: that is SfM. VO and visual SLAM process a video stream as it arrives.

Q4. Why do direct methods usually start on down-sampled images?

Brightness gradients guide the optimizer only near the solution. Coarse levels bring a large displacement within reach, and finer levels then refine it.

Q5. Which component removes accumulated drift in a visual SLAM system that has no GPS?

Odometry errors accumulate no matter how good each step is. Aligning the current image to previously observed ones gives constraints across the loop, and the back-end redistributes the error.

Recap

  1. Bundle adjustment predates computers; visual SLAM is its online, causal, real-time descendant.
  2. SfM is offline reconstruction, VO is local motion estimation that drifts, and visual SLAM adds place recognition and loop closure.
  3. A camera does not measure depth. From two views, \(z = f\,b/d\) and \(\sigma_z \approx z^2\sigma_d/(f\,b)\).
  4. A visual SLAM system has an odometry front-end, a mapping back-end, and a place recognition and relocalization component, running in parallel.
  5. Feature-based odometry minimizes reprojection error with explicit matches; direct odometry minimizes a photometric loss and needs coarse-to-fine to converge.
