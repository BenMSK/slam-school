Week 10 review, and Part I in one page

After today you can extend certifiable solvers beyond pose graphs, bound the accuracy of a SLAM estimate from its graph, and place every tool of Part I in one picture of the back-end.

About 75 min · No new pages

The week in one paragraph

The first half of the week pushed the certifiable recipe of Week 9 outward. Landmarks fit as poses without rotation. Ranges fit after adding a unit vector per measurement. Anisotropic noise and robust losses give polynomial problems of degree 4 and 3, which the moment relaxation turns into SDPs that certify but are slow to solve. The second half asked how accurate even the optimal estimate can be. The Cramér-Rao bound says the covariance of an unbiased estimator is at least the inverse Fisher information \((\mathbf{J}\T\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathbf{J})^{-1}\). For a position graph with known rotations that information is the reduced weighted Laplacian, so accuracy is a matter of connectivity: spanning trees, algebraic connectivity, effective resistance.

2. Mixed quiz: Week 10

Check yourself

Q1. A landmark SLAM problem has 50 poses and 400 landmarks. After SE-Sync eliminates all positions, what remains to optimize?

Landmarks behave like pose translations and are eliminated with them. Only the 50 rotations remain.

Q2. The difference of two positions is v = (0, 5) and the measured range is 2. The best unit vector b and the value of ‖v − 2b‖² are:

b points along v, and the value is (‖v‖ − r)² = (5 − 2)² = 9.

Q3. Which pairing of problem and polynomial degree of the objective is right?

A weight times a squared residual is cubic. uᵀ(R W⁻¹ Rᵀ)u is quartic. The range trick restores degree 2.

Q4. For one unknown x and order r = 3, the monomial vector is [1; x; x²; x³]. Up to which degree can a polynomial be written as a linear function of the moment matrix?

The entries of the outer product are products of two monomials of degree up to 3, so degrees up to 6 appear.

Q5. Four independent measurements z = x + noise, each with σ = 0.2. What is the smallest standard deviation an unbiased estimator of x can have?

Variance bound σ²/N = 0.04/4 = 0.01, so the standard deviation is 0.1.

Q6. The FIM is [[4, 2], [2, 2]]. What is the Cramér-Rao bound on the variance of the first variable?

The inverse is (1/4)[[2, −2], [−2, 4]], whose first diagonal entry is 0.5. Not 1/4: invert first, then read the diagonal.

Q7. A chain 0–1–2 with pose 0 anchored has edge weights 4 (between 0 and 1) and 1 (between 1 and 2). What is the variance per axis of pose 2?

Resistances 1/4 and 1 in series: 0.25 + 1 = 1.25.

Q8. A square (a 4-cycle with unit weights) has 4 spanning trees. You add a unit-weight diagonal. How many spanning trees are there now?

Opposite corners are joined by two paths of resistance 2, so R = 1. The determinant is multiplied by 1 + 1·1 = 2: 8 trees.

3. Pen-and-paper problems

  1. A 1-D chain 0–1–2 has pose 0 anchored, weight 2 on edge 0–1 and weight 1 on edge 1–2. (a) Write the reduced Laplacian and the variance of each pose. (b) Add a loop closure 2–0 of weight 1 and repeat. (c) Check the determinants against the weighted spanning-tree count and against \(\log(1+wR_{ij})\).

    Solution

    (a) \(\mathbf{L}_w=\begin{bmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\), \(\det=2\), \(\mathbf{L}_w^{-1}=\tfrac12\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{bmatrix}\). Variances 0.5 and 1.5 (resistances \(\tfrac12\) and \(\tfrac12+1\) in series).

    (b) \(\mathbf{L}_w=\begin{bmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\), \(\det=5\), \(\mathbf{L}_w^{-1}=\tfrac15\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{bmatrix}\). Variances 0.4 and 0.6. Check pose 2: paths of resistance 1 and 1.5 in parallel give \(1.5/2.5=0.6\).

    (c) Trees: \(\{01,12\}\) weighs \(2\), \(\{01,20\}\) weighs \(2\), \(\{12,20\}\) weighs \(1\); total 5. Before the closure \(R_{20}=1.5\), so the determinant is multiplied by \(1+1\cdot 1.5=2.5\): \(2\times 2.5=5\).

  2. A robot at \(x=4\) on a line measures its range to beacon A at \((0,3)\) and to beacon B at \((8,3)\), each with \(\sigma=0.1\) m. (a) What is the Fisher information for \(x\), and the smallest standard deviation of an unbiased estimate? (b) Repeat with \(\sigma_B=0.2\) m.

    Solution

    (a) \(h_A=\sqrt{x^2+9}\), \(J_A=4/5=0.8\). \(h_B=\sqrt{(x-8)^2+9}\), \(J_B=(4-8)/5=-0.8\). \(\mathcal{I}=\mathbf{J}\T\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathbf{J}=(0.64+0.64)/0.01=128\). Standard deviation at least \(1/\sqrt{128}=0.088\) m.

    (b) \(\mathcal{I}=0.64/0.01+0.64/0.04=64+16=80\), so at least \(1/\sqrt{80}=0.112\) m. The noisier beacon still adds information; it never hurts.

  3. For \(p(x)=x^4-2x^2+3\), write \(p(x)-\gamma=[1\ x\ x^2]\,\mathbf{Q}\,[1\ x\ x^2]\T\) with a free parameter \(\lambda\). Find the largest \(\gamma\) for which \(\mathbf{Q}\succeq 0\) is possible, and confirm that it is the global minimum.

    Solution

    \(\mathbf{Q}=\begin{bmatrix} 3-\gamma & 0 & -\lambda \\ 0 & 2\lambda-2 & 0 \\ -\lambda & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\); the \(x^2\) coefficient is \((2\lambda-2)-2\lambda=-2\). The middle entry needs \(\lambda\ge 1\). The corner block \(\begin{bmatrix} 3-\gamma & -\lambda \\ -\lambda & 1 \end{bmatrix}\) is positive semidefinite when its determinant is non-negative: \(3-\gamma-\lambda^2\ge 0\), that is \(\gamma\le 3-\lambda^2\). The best choice is \(\lambda=1\), giving \(\gamma=2\).

    Check: \(p(x)-2=x^4-2x^2+1=(x^2-1)^2\ge 0\), with equality at \(x=\pm 1\), where \(p=2\). The bound equals the global minimum, so the relaxation is exact.

  4. (Stretch.) The Sherman-Morrison formula says \((\mathbf{L}+w\,\mathbf{a}\mathbf{a}\T)^{-1}=\mathbf{L}^{-1}-\dfrac{w\,\mathbf{L}^{-1}\mathbf{a}\mathbf{a}\T\mathbf{L}^{-1}}{1+w\,\mathbf{a}\T\mathbf{L}^{-1}\mathbf{a}}\). (a) Use it to show that a new edge never increases any pose's variance. (b) Apply it to the chain 0–1–2–3 with unit weights and the new unit-weight edge 3–0, and recover the variances 0.75, 1, 0.75 of Day 4.

    Solution

    (a) The \(i\)-th diagonal entry of the subtracted matrix is \(\dfrac{w\,([\mathbf{L}^{-1}\mathbf{a}]_i)^2}{1+wR}\) with \(R=\mathbf{a}\T\mathbf{L}^{-1}\mathbf{a}\ge 0\). It is a square over a positive number, so it is non-negative, and each variance can only go down.

    (b) \(\mathbf{L}^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 2 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}\), and the edge from 3 to the anchor has \(\mathbf{a}=[0;0;-1]\). Then \(\mathbf{L}^{-1}\mathbf{a}=-[1;2;3]\) and \(R=3\). The reductions are \(\tfrac{1}{4},\tfrac{4}{4},\tfrac{9}{4}\), so the new variances are \(1-0.25=0.75\), \(2-1=1\), \(3-2.25=0.75\).

5. Part I quiz

Check yourself: Weeks 1–10

Q1. A prior on x is Gaussian with mean 0 and variance 4. One measurement z = 5 of x has variance 1. What is the posterior mean?

In information form: precision 1/4 + 1 = 1.25, and mean (0·1/4 + 5·1)/1.25 = 4. The estimate sits close to the more precise source.

Q2. In Levenberg-Marquardt, what happens to the step as the damping parameter grows large?

With (JᵀJ + λI)δ = −Jᵀr and large λ, δ ≈ −Jᵀr/λ: a small steepest-descent step. Small λ gives Gauss-Newton.

Q3. In a chain x₁ – x₂ – x₃, you eliminate (marginalize out) x₂ first. What appears in the remaining problem?

The Schur complement couples all neighbours of the eliminated variable. A good elimination order keeps such fill-in small.

Q4. Why does the book update a pose as T = T⁰ Exp(ξ) and not as T⁰ + Δ?

SE(3) is a group, not a vector space. Optimizing over the 6-vector ξ keeps the constraints satisfied without enforcing them.

Q5. Why is Gaussian-process regression for trajectories cheap when the prior comes from a linear stochastic differential equation such as white noise on acceleration?

Each state depends only on its neighbours in time, which gives a sparse precision matrix and a factor graph with binary factors.

Q6. RANSAC fits a model from 3 points per sample. Half the data are inliers. Roughly how many samples give a 99% chance of drawing at least one all-inlier sample?

An all-inlier sample has probability 0.5³ = 0.125. Solve (1 − 0.125)ᴺ ≤ 0.01: N ≥ ln 0.01 / ln 0.875 ≈ 34.5, so 35.

Q7. Compared with unrolling K solver iterations, what is the main advantage of implicit differentiation through an optimizer?

Unrolling stores every iterate for the backward pass. The implicit function theorem uses the optimality condition at the solution only, and assumes the solver has converged.

Q8. An occupancy cell starts at probability 0.5. Two independent range readings each say "occupied" with inverse sensor model 0.7. What is the cell's probability after the log-odds updates?

Odds multiply: (0.7/0.3)² = 5.44, and 5.44/6.44 = 0.845. In log-odds, 2 × ln(7/3) = 1.69.

Q9. Shor's relaxation of a QCQP returns an optimal matrix X* of rank 1. What follows?

A rank-1 solution is feasible for the original problem and attains the lower bound d*, so p* = d*.

Q10. A SLAM estimate is certified globally optimal, yet the far end of the trajectory is 2 m from the truth. Which explanation fits Part I?

A certificate is about the cost, not about the truth. Accuracy is limited by the data, and for a chain the variance grows with distance from the anchor.
