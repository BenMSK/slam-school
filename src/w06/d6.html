Week 6 review: outliers and robust estimation

After today you can choose between RANSAC, pairwise consistency, robust losses, and graduated non-convexity for a given problem, and carry out one step of each by hand.

About 60 min · No new pages

The week in one paragraph

Least squares assumes Gaussian errors, and one grossly wrong measurement, usually a wrong data association, can move its answer arbitrarily far. Chapter 3 defends in two layers. In the front-end, RANSAC samples minimal sets and keeps the model most measurements agree with, and pairwise consistency maximization keeps the largest group of measurements that agree with each other, found as a maximum clique. In the back-end, M-estimation swaps the square for a robust loss, IRLS solves it as repeated weighted least squares, Black-Rangarajan duality explains the weights, and graduated non-convexity makes IRLS far less dependent on its starting point. None of these comes with a guarantee, and the strongest results combine the layers.

2. Mixed quiz

Check yourself

Q1. Four measurements of a distance read 10 m and a fifth reads 60 m. What does least squares return?

The mean: (4 × 10 + 60)/5 = 20 m. Equivalently, the 50 m error is divided by N = 5, a shift of 10 m.

Q2. A minimal solver needs n = 3 correspondences and half of the correspondences are inliers. What is the expected number of RANSAC iterations before an all-inlier sample?

1/ωⁿ = 1/0.5³ = 8.

Q3. 30% of the matches are correct, and 45% are wrong but all caused by the same repeated structure, so they agree with one another. What does RANSAC most likely return?

RANSAC maximizes consensus. It cannot tell a large self-consistent group of wrong matches from the truth. This is perceptual aliasing at the level of the front-end.

Q4. Two matched 3D points are 2.00 m apart in frame k − 1 and 2.05 m apart in frame k. With γ = 0.10 m, what does the consistency test (3.5) say?

|2.00 − 2.05| = 0.05 ≤ 0.10, so the pair gets an edge in the consistency graph. The test needs no motion estimate. Passing it is necessary for both to be inliers, not sufficient.

Q5. A consistency graph has edges 1–2, 1–3, 2–3, 3–4, 4–5. Which measurements does PCM keep?

{1, 2, 3} is the maximum clique: all three pairs are edges. {3, 4, 5} is not a clique because 3–5 is missing. Node 3 has the most edges, but degree alone does not decide.

Q6. Geman-McClure loss with β = 1. What IRLS weight does a residual r = 3 get?

w = (β²/(β² + r²))² = (1/10)² = 0.01. Small, but not exactly zero as it would be for the truncated quadratic.

Q7. Which of these methods needs an initial guess of the state?

RANSAC builds its own estimates from minimal samples and PCM never touches the state. IRLS and GNC run Gauss-Newton-type steps on the full problem, and GNC in particular fails when the initial guess is broken (Figure 3.10).

Q8. In GNC with the truncated quadratic surrogate (3.24), what happens to the band of residuals with weights strictly between 0 and 1 as μ grows?

The band is r² ∈ [μ/(μ+1) β², (μ+1)/μ β²]. Both ends tend to β² as μ → ∞, so the surrogate becomes min{r², β²} and the weights become binary.

3. Pen-and-paper problems

  1. A 3D-3D registration front-end uses a 3-point minimal solver. (a) With 60% outliers, find the expected number of RANSAC iterations to draw an all-inlier sample, and the number needed for 99% confidence. (b) Repeat for 90% outliers.

    Solution

    (a) \(\omega = 0.4\), \(\omega^3 = 0.064\). Expected: \(1/0.064 = 15.6\). For 99%: \(k \ge \log(0.01)/\log(1-0.064) = (-4.605)/(-0.0661) = 69.6\), so 70 iterations.

    (b) \(\omega = 0.1\), \(\omega^3 = 0.001\). Expected: 1000. For 99%: \((-4.605)/\log(0.999) = (-4.605)/(-0.0010005) = 4603\) iterations. Going from 60% to 90% outliers costs a factor of about 65, which is why the chapter turns to PCM for very high outlier rates.

  2. A robot drives down a corridor and back (treat it as one-dimensional). Five candidate loop closures each link an early pose \(a_i\) to a late pose \(b_i\). For each one you compute \(e_i\) = (loop-closure displacement) − (displacement predicted by chaining odometry from \(a_i\) to \(b_i\)), and get \(e = \{0.42,\ 0.38,\ 2.90,\ 0.45,\ -1.70\}\) m. (a) Show that the cycle test (3.6) for loop closures \(i\) and \(j\) reduces to \(|e_i - e_j|\le\gamma\). (b) With \(\gamma = 0.15\) m, build the consistency graph and find the maximum clique. (c) What do the kept loop closures say about the odometry?

    Solution

    (a) In one dimension poses compose by addition and the identity is 0. Go around the cycle \(a_i \to b_i \to b_j \to a_j \to a_i\): loop closure \(i\) forwards (\(T_i\)), odometry from \(b_i\) to \(b_j\), loop closure \(j\) backwards (\(-T_j\)), odometry from \(a_j\) to \(a_i\). Writing \(q\) for positions obtained by chaining odometry, the sum is \(T_i + (q_{b_j}-q_{b_i}) - T_j + (q_{a_i}-q_{a_j}) = [T_i-(q_{b_i}-q_{a_i})] - [T_j-(q_{b_j}-q_{a_j})] = e_i - e_j\).

    (b) There are \(5\times4/2 = 10\) pairs. \(|e_1-e_2| = 0.04\), \(|e_1-e_4| = 0.03\), \(|e_2-e_4| = 0.07\): three edges. Every pair involving closure 3 or closure 5 differs by more than 2 m: no edge. The graph is the triangle {1, 2, 4} plus two isolated nodes, so the maximum clique is {1, 2, 4}.

    (c) They agree that odometry drifted by about 0.4 m between the two passes. Closures 3 and 5 each imply a different drift that nothing else supports.

  3. Estimate a scalar \(x\) from \(z = \{0, 1, 2, 3, 19\}\) with residuals \(r_i = z_i - x\). (a) Give the least-squares estimate. (b) Run IRLS with the truncated quadratic loss, \(\beta = 3.5\), from the least-squares estimate until it stops changing. (c) Find the Huber estimate for \(\beta = 3.5\) from the condition \(\sum_i\psi(r_i) = 0\).

    Solution

    (a) The mean: \(25/5 = 5\).

    (b) Iteration 1: residuals \(-5, -4, -3, -2, 14\). Weights are 1 where \(|r|\lt3.5\): \(0, 0, 1, 1, 0\). New \(x = (2+3)/2 = 2.5\). Iteration 2: residuals \(-2.5, -1.5, -0.5, 0.5, 16.5\), weights \(1, 1, 1, 1, 0\), new \(x = 6/4 = 1.5\). Iteration 3: residuals \(-1.5, -0.5, 0.5, 1.5, 17.5\), same weights, \(x = 1.5\). Converged at the mean of the four good values. Here the bad start did not matter. Compare the Day 4 widget, where it did.

    (c) Guess that the first four residuals are within \(\beta\) and the last is beyond it. Then \(\psi = 2r\) for the first four and \(\psi = 2\beta\) for the last: \(2[(0-x)+(1-x)+(2-x)+(3-x)] + 2(3.5) = 0\), so \(12 - 8x + 7 = 0\) and \(x = 2.375\). Check the guess: the residuals are \(-2.375, -1.375, -0.375, 0.625\) (all within 3.5) and \(16.625\) (beyond). The Huber estimate is 2.375, between the truncated-quadratic answer 1.5 and the least-squares answer 5, because the outlier keeps a constant pull of \(2\beta\).

  4. (Stretch.) For GNC with the truncated quadratic, the outlier process is \(\Phi_{\rho_\mu}(w) = \dfrac{\mu(1-w)}{\mu+w}\beta^2\) and the weight inside the band is \(w^\star = \beta\sqrt{\mu(\mu+1)}/|r| - \mu\). (a) Substitute \(w^\star\) into \(w\,r^2 + \Phi_{\rho_\mu}(w)\) and show that you get the middle case of (3.24). (b) Evaluate \(w^\star\) and \(\rho_\mu\) for \(\beta = 2,\ \mu = 0.25,\ r = 3\).

    Solution

    (a) Let \(s = \beta\sqrt{\mu(\mu+1)}\), so \(w^\star = s/|r| - \mu\), \(\mu + w^\star = s/|r|\), and \(s^2 = \mu(\mu+1)\beta^2\). First term: \(w^\star r^2 = s|r| - \mu r^2\). Second term: \(\mu\beta^2\,\dfrac{1+\mu-s/|r|}{s/|r|} = \dfrac{\mu(\mu+1)\beta^2\,|r|}{s} - \mu\beta^2 = s|r| - \mu\beta^2\). The sum is \(2s|r| - \mu(\beta^2+r^2) = 2\beta|r|\sqrt{\mu(\mu+1)} - \mu(\beta^2+r^2)\), the middle case of (3.24).

    (b) The band is \(r^2\in[\tfrac{0.25}{1.25}\cdot4,\ \tfrac{1.25}{0.25}\cdot4] = [0.8, 20]\), and \(r^2 = 9\) is inside. \(\sqrt{\mu(\mu+1)} = \sqrt{0.3125} = 0.559\). \(w^\star = 2(0.559)/3 - 0.25 = 0.1227\). \(\rho_\mu = 2(2)(3)(0.559) - 0.25(4+9) = 6.708 - 3.25 = 3.458\). Check through the weight: \(0.1227\times9 + 0.25\times4\times\tfrac{0.8773}{0.3727} = 1.104 + 2.354 = 3.458\). The plain truncated quadratic would give weight 0 and loss 4.
