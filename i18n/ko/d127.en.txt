A <strong>metric-semantic map</strong> stores what things are together with where they are. In its sparse form, the landmarks stop being anonymous 3D points and become objects with a category, a pose, a scale, and a shape. The back-end stays the same least-squares machine; only the residual changes.

3. Shape as a set of semantic landmarks
@@ 44
The first shape model is a short list of 3D points \(\{\mathbf{s}_j\}_j\), called <strong>semantic landmarks</strong>, placed on named parts of the object: for a car, the wheels, mirrors, and plate. Their projections into an image are <strong>semantic keypoints</strong> \(\mathbf{z}_j \in \R^2\). A network detects them by producing, for each part, a heatmap with a bump at the part's pixel. <span class="src">§16.2.1.1 · p. 457</span>
@@ 45
If every \(\mathbf{s}_j\) were free, the optimizer could move the left wheel to the roof. The <strong>deformable shape model</strong> prevents that by building the points from a category template:
@@ 46
Here \(\mathbf{b}_{j,0}\) is the position of part \(j\) on the average car, each \(\mathbf{b}_{j,k}\) is one way cars differ from the average (a <strong>mode of variability</strong>), and the <strong>deformation coefficients</strong> \(c_k \in \R_{\ge 0}\) say how much of each mode this car has. The \(\mathbf{b}\)'s are fixed per category. The \(c_k\) are estimated per object. <span class="src">§16.2.1.1 · p. 457</span>

@@ 48
Notice that shape alone cannot explain the detections: with the wrong scale and yaw, \(c_1\) runs into its upper limit and the error stays above 130 cm. Three Gauss-Newton steps on all four unknowns bring it to about 6 cm, close to the noise level, at \(c_1 \approx 0.80\), \(c_2 \approx 0.53\), \(\lambda \approx 1.29\), yaw 25°; the fourth changes nothing.

Filling the gap: where the modes \(\mathbf{b}_{j,k}\) come from
@@ 50
The book says the modes are obtained by PCA. The recipe: take \(N\) CAD models of the category, each annotated with the same \(J\) parts. Stack the \(J\) part positions of model \(n\) into one vector \(\mathbf{m}_n \in \R^{3J}\). Then
@@ 51
The mean \(\bar{\mathbf{m}}\), cut back into \(J\) triples, gives the \(\mathbf{b}_{j,0}\). The eigenvectors of \(\mathbf{C}\) with the largest eigenvalues are the directions in which the training cars differ most; cutting eigenvector \(k\) into triples gives the \(\mathbf{b}_{j,k}\).
@@ 52
Count the unknowns. With \(J = 12\) parts, free points cost \(36\) numbers per car. With \(K = 5\) modes the shape costs \(5\), and the estimate stays car-shaped even when half the keypoints are hidden.

Filling the gap: reading \(\pi(\mathbf{q}^{-1}\mathbf{T}, \lambda\mathbf{s})\), with numbers
@@ 65
Both \(\mathbf{T}\) (camera) and \(\mathbf{q}\) (object) map their own frame to the world frame. So \(\mathbf{q}^{-1}\mathbf{T}\) maps camera coordinates to object coordinates: it is the pose of the camera as seen from the object. To project a point given in object coordinates you need the opposite direction, \((\mathbf{q}^{-1}\mathbf{T})^{-1} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{q}\), followed by the pinhole model of <a href="#d63">Week 11 · Day 3</a>.
@@ 66
Take a camera at the world origin with \(\mathbf{T} = \mathbf{I}\), focal length 500 px, principal point \((320, 240)\). The object frame is not rotated and sits at \((1, 0, 8)\) m. Its scale is \(\lambda = 2\), and one semantic landmark is \(\mathbf{s} = (0.5, 0.25, 0)\).
@@ 67
If the detector reports \(\mathbf{z} = (448, 270)\), the error is \(\mathbf{e} = (3, -1.25)\) px. With \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma} = 2^2\mathbf{I}\) px² this part contributes \((3^2 + 1.25^2)/4 = 2.64\) to \(r_i^2\). The optimizer can lower it through \(\mathbf{T}\), \(\mathbf{q}\), \(\lambda\), or the shape coefficients.
@@ 68
<strong>"\(\mathbf{q}\) takes world coordinates to object coordinates."</strong> It is the pose of the object frame in the world, so it takes object coordinates to world coordinates. That is why (16.3) contains \(\mathbf{q}^{-1}\mathbf{T}\) and not \(\mathbf{q}\mathbf{T}\).
