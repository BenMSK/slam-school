1. The gap between the task and the budget
@@ 11
Chapter 18, by Andrew Davison, has no equations and argues for a position. <strong>Spatial AI</strong> is the capability of a device to build rich but efficient representations of its surroundings that let it act intelligently. The aim is the right representation for real-time interpretation and action; scene understanding in the abstract is not the goal. <span class="src">§18.1.1 · p. 521</span> A design is squeezed between what the task needs and what the device can carry. <span class="src">§18.1.1 · pp. 521–522</span>

2. Representation, also called a world model
@@ 23
A <strong>representation</strong> is the set of values in memory that stand for the state of the device and the world around it. It persists, it changes as information arrives or the world changes, and parts of it may be abstracted or forgotten to save resources. <span class="src">§18.1.2 · p. 522</span> Machine learning calls this a <strong>world model</strong>. The idea looks new there because most networks are <strong>stateless</strong>: input in, output out, nothing kept. Networks trained <strong>end to end</strong> from sensor to action do well on short tasks and struggle on long ones. A map is the oldest fix: a robot vacuum can guarantee full coverage because it remembers where it has been. <span class="src">§18.1.2 · pp. 522–523</span>
@@ 24
<strong>Hypothesis 1.</strong> A device that must run for a long time, do many tasks (some unknown at design time), and communicate with people should keep a scene representation that is general, persistent, close to metric 3D geometry at least locally, and understandable by a human. This excludes single-task representations and opaque learned states. The reasons given are efficiency, composability, interpretability, and generality. <span class="src">§18.1.2 · p. 523</span>
@@ 25
<strong>Hypothesis 2.</strong> The usefulness of such a system across many tasks is captured by a small number of performance measures, so a drone, a tidying robot, and an AR display would run largely the same system, tuned by a few numbers. <span class="src">§18.1.2 · pp. 523–524</span> <a href="#d143">Day 5</a> returns to this.
@@ 26
A <strong>Spatial AI system</strong>, for the chapter, is a module that builds and maintains such a representation incrementally, in real time, from mainly visual input, with quantifiable performance. <span class="src">§18.1.2 · p. 524</span>

@@ 50
The quality the chapter singles out is the <strong>closed loop</strong>: the model is persistent, updated incrementally, and used inside the real-time loop for data association and tracking. The contrast is <strong>incremental estimation</strong> such as pure visual odometry, which estimates motion frame to frame and keeps no long-term structure. Closed loop does not mean one fixed rate for everything. It means each result is available when needed, so the whole never pauses. <span class="src">§18.2 · pp. 525–526</span>

Filling the gap: why the stored map bounds the error
@@ 69
In the widget, a landmark at \(l_j\) seen from position \(x_k\) gives \(z_{kj} = l_j - x_k + n_{kj}\), with independent noise of variance \(\sigma^2\).
@@ 70
<em>Odometry</em> sums frame-to-frame motions, each the average change in \(z\) over the landmarks two frames share. While the shared set is unchanged the noise cancels in the sum. Each time a landmark enters or leaves the view, a term fails to cancel and a fresh error, a fraction of \(\sigma\) in size, is added for good. After \(h\) hand-overs the error variance has grown in proportion to \(h\): the random walk of <a href="#d1">Week 1 · Day 1</a>.
@@ 71
<em>The stored map</em> holds landmark \(j\) as \(\hat{l}_j = l_j + e_j\), where \(e_j\) is the error at the moment it was stored. On any later frame with \(m\) landmarks in view,
@@ 72
The first error term is frozen: it is the same on every return to those landmarks. The second is fresh noise of variance \(\sigma^2/m\). Neither depends on how long the system has run. With \(\sigma = 0.05\) and \(m \approx 6\) the fresh part is \(0.05/\sqrt{6} \approx 0.02\); the rest of the 0.04 is map error frozen in during the first sweep.
