@@ 3
After today you can go from the IMU measurement model to a preintegrated factor by hand on a small example, and estimate how fast uncorrected inertial errors grow.
@@ 4
An IMU reports the body's rotation rate and its specific force (acceleration minus gravity) in body axes, each with a slowly drifting bias and white noise. Standing still, the specific force points up, which fixes roll and pitch but not yaw. Integrating the readings gives rotation, velocity, and position, but errors grow quickly: quadratically for an accelerometer bias and cubically for a gyroscope bias, because a tilt error leaks gravity into horizontal acceleration. To use the IMU in a factor graph without a state per sample, the integration between two keyframes is rearranged into increments that do not depend on the starting state or on gravity. They are integrated once, corrected to first order when the bias estimate moves, and enter the graph as one 9-dimensional factor plus a bias random-walk factor.

A car on a level road brakes at 3 m/s². Its IMU has x forward and z up. (a) What does the accelerometer read, ignoring bias and noise? (b) If an alignment routine wrongly assumed the car was at rest, what pitch would it report?
@@ 60
(a) \(\mathbf{a}^w = (-3, 0, 0)\), \(\mathbf{g}^w = (0,0,-9.81)\), \(\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{I}\). The reading is \(\mathbf{a}^w - \mathbf{g}^w = (-3, 0, 9.81)\) m/s², of length \(\sqrt{9 + 96.24} = 10.26\) m/s².
@@ 61
(b) The routine takes the reading for "up". It is tilted from the z-axis toward \(-x\) by \(\arctan(3/9.81) = 17.0^\circ\), so the car appears pitched nose-down by 17°. The length 10.26 instead of 9.81 is the giveaway that it was not at rest.

An IMU has an uncorrected gyroscope bias of 0.01°/s and an uncorrected accelerometer bias of 0.02 m/s². After how long does the position error from the gyroscope bias overtake the one from the accelerometer bias, and how large are they then?
@@ 67
Set \(\tfrac16 g\,b_g t^3 = \tfrac12 b_a t^2\), so \(t = 3b_a/(g\,b_g)\). With \(b_g = 0.01^\circ/\text{s} = 1.745\times10^{-4}\) rad/s: \(t = 0.06/(9.81\times1.745\times10^{-4}) = 35.0\) s.
@@ 68
At that moment each error is \(\tfrac12\times0.02\times35.0^2 = 12.3\) m. Before 35 s the accelerometer bias dominates; afterwards the cubic term wins and keeps pulling ahead.

A 1-D preintegration by hand. A sensor moves along a vertical axis without rotating, z up, \(g = -9.81\) m/s². Three accelerometer samples at \(\Delta t = 0.1\) s read 10.81, 9.81, 8.81 m/s², with zero bias. (a) Compute \(\Delta v_{ij}\) and \(\Delta p_{ij}\). (b) Predict \(v_j\) and \(p_j\) for \(v_i = 1\) m/s, \(p_i = 0\), and check by direct integration with (11.12). (c) The solver changes \(v_i\) to 1.5 m/s. What is \(p_j\) now? (d) The accelerometer bias estimate changes from 0 to 0.1 m/s². Correct \(\Delta v_{ij}\) and \(\Delta p_{ij}\) to first order and compare with re-integration.
@@ 70
(a) With no rotation \(\Delta R = 1\). \(\Delta v_{ij} = (10.81 + 9.81 + 8.81)\times0.1 = 2.943\) m/s. For \(\Delta p_{ij} = \sum_k[\Delta v_{ik}\Delta t + \tfrac12\tilde a_k\Delta t^2]\) the running \(\Delta v_{ik}\) are 0, 1.081, 2.062:
@@ 71
\(k=0\): \(0 + 0.05405\); \(k=1\): \(0.1081 + 0.04905\); \(k=2\): \(0.2062 + 0.04405\). Sum: \(\Delta p_{ij} = 0.46145\) m.
@@ 72
Direct: the true accelerations are \(\tilde a + g = 1, 0, -1\). Velocity: 1 → 1.1 → 1.1 → 1.0. Position: \(0.1 + 0.005 = 0.105\); \(+0.11 = 0.215\); \(+0.11 - 0.005 = 0.32\). They agree.
@@ 73
(c) Only the \(v_i\Delta t_{ij}\) term changes: \(p_j = 0.45 - 0.44145 + 0.46145 = 0.47\) m. No sample is touched.
@@ 74
(d) Without rotation the Jacobians are \(\partial\Delta v/\partial b^a = -\Delta t_{ij} = -0.3\) s and \(\partial\Delta p/\partial b^a = -\tfrac12\Delta t_{ij}^2 = -0.045\) s². Corrected: \(\Delta v_{ij} = 2.943 - 0.03 = 2.913\) m/s, \(\Delta p_{ij} = 0.46145 - 0.0045 = 0.45695\) m. Re-integrating with samples 10.71, 9.71, 8.71 gives \(\Delta v = 2.913\) and \(\Delta p = 0.05355 + (0.1071 + 0.04855) + (0.2042 + 0.04355) = 0.45695\): identical, because the recipes are linear in the accelerometer bias.
