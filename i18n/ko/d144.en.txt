@@ 4
Chapter 18 argues that Spatial AI is SLAM continued: a device keeps a persistent, close-to-metric world model and checks every measurement against it in a closed loop. Because products limit size, cost, and power, and because single-core speed stopped rising when Dennard scaling failed, the computation has to be spread over many simple units with little data movement. The design gives each graph in the problem matching hardware: a self-maintaining map store, a real-time loop, and processing at the sensor. Gaussian belief propagation is the estimation algorithm that fits, since it needs only local messages and tolerates any schedule, and it can carry learning in the same factor graph. Success is to be judged on a dozen metrics at once, read as trade-offs.

Q2. A camera tracks against a stored map with 9 landmarks in view and measurement noise σ = 0.06. What is the standard deviation of the fresh noise in its position estimate?
@@ 37
Averaging 9 independent measurements divides σ by √9: 0.06/3 = 0.02.

Q3. An estimate is stored as (η, Λ) = (40, 20). A new measurement z = 5 with variance 0.2 arrives. What is the new mean?
@@ 39
The measurement adds Λ = 1/0.2 = 5 and η = 5/0.2 = 25. New pair: (65, 25), mean 65/25 = 2.6.

@@ 41

@@ 58
Three poses on a line. Prior: \(x_0 = 0\), variance 0.04. Odometry: \(x_1 - x_0 = 1.0\) and \(x_2 - x_1 = 1.2\), each with variance 0.09. Run GBP by hand and give the mean and variance of each belief. Why do the messages travelling back toward \(x_0\) carry nothing?
@@ 59
The prior gives \(x_0\): mean 0, variance 0.04. Through the first odometry factor the mean gains the step and the variances add: \(x_1\) has mean 1.0, variance \(0.04 + 0.09 = 0.13\). Through the second: \(x_2\) has mean 2.2, variance \(0.13 + 0.09 = 0.22\).
@@ 60
The message from \(x_2\) to the second odometry factor is \(x_2\)'s belief minus what that factor sent, and \(x_2\) has no other factor, so it is zero. A factor that receives zero information sends zero onward (\(\Lambda = w\cdot 0/(w+0) = 0\)). With no measurement downstream there is nothing to report upstream.
@@ 61
Add a position fix to Problem 1: \(x_2 = 2.0\) with variance 0.02. Compute the new beliefs of \(x_2\) and \(x_1\).
@@ 62
<em>Belief of \(x_2\).</em> Two incoming messages: from odometry (mean 2.2, variance 0.22), so \((\eta,\Lambda) = (10,\ 4.545)\); from the fix, \((100,\ 50)\). Sum: \((110,\ 54.545)\). Mean \(110/54.545 = 2.017\), variance \(1/54.545 = 0.0183\).
@@ 63
<em>Message back to \(x_1\).</em> \(x_2\) sends the second odometry factor its belief minus that factor's message, which leaves the fix: mean 2.0, variance 0.02. The factor passes it on with the step subtracted and the variances added: mean \(2.0 - 1.2 = 0.8\), variance \(0.02 + 0.09 = 0.11\).
@@ 64
<em>Belief of \(x_1\).</em> Forward message (mean 1.0, variance 0.13) is \((7.692,\ 7.692)\); backward message (mean 0.8, variance 0.11) is \((7.273,\ 9.091)\). Sum: \((14.965,\ 16.783)\). Mean \(0.892\), variance \(0.0596\). Solving the \(3\times3\) system \(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\boldsymbol{\mu} = \boldsymbol{\eta}\) directly gives the same numbers, because the graph has no loop.
@@ 65
Four pixel processors sit in a ring, each linked to its two neighbours, and hold 0, 4, 8, 4. Using the averaging rule with \(w_{ij} = 1/(1+\max(d_i,d_j))\), find the values after one round, and the number of rounds until every pixel is within 0.05 of the mean.
@@ 66
Every pixel has degree 2, so every weight is \(1/3\). The mean is \(16/4 = 4\).
@@ 67
Round 1: pixel 1 becomes \(0 + \tfrac13(4-0) + \tfrac13(4-0) = 2.67\). Pixel 3 becomes \(8 + \tfrac13(4-8) + \tfrac13(4-8) = 5.33\). Pixels 2 and 4 see one neighbour 4 below and one 4 above and stay at 4. The values are 2.67, 4, 5.33, 4, and the sum is still 16.
@@ 68
The deviations from the mean went from \((-4, 0, 4, 0)\) to \((-1.33, 0, 1.33, 0)\): each round multiplies them by \(1/3\). After \(t\) rounds the largest deviation is \(4/3^t\). It is \(4/27 = 0.148\) at \(t = 3\) and \(4/81 = 0.049\) at \(t = 4\). Four rounds.
@@ 69
(Stretch.) Show with numbers that one forward step of GBP on a pose chain is one predict-and-update step of a Kalman filter. Prior: \(x_0\) has mean 0, variance 1. Odometry: \(x_1 - x_0 = 1\), variance 1. A position fix: \(x_1 = 3\), variance 1.
@@ 70
<em>Kalman filter.</em> Predict: mean \(0 + 1 = 1\), variance \(1 + 1 = 2\). Update with the fix: gain \(K = 2/(2+1) = 2/3\), mean \(1 + \tfrac23(3 - 1) = 2.33\), variance \((1 - \tfrac23)\cdot 2 = 0.667\).
@@ 71
<em>GBP.</em> The odometry factor's message to \(x_1\) is the prediction: mean 1, variance 2, so \((\eta,\Lambda) = (0.5,\ 0.5)\). The fix sends \((3,\ 1)\). The belief is the sum, \((3.5,\ 1.5)\): mean \(3.5/1.5 = 2.33\), variance \(1/1.5 = 0.667\).
@@ 72
The factor-to-variable message is the predict step, and adding the fix's message is the update step written in information form. Messages sent backward along the chain then turn the filter into a smoother. Compare <a href="#d3">Week 1 · Day 3</a>.

Q1 (Week 2). With Gaussian noise on every factor, what is MAP inference on a factor graph equivalent to?

Q2 (Week 3). Why can a poor elimination order make sparse Cholesky slow?

@@ 158

@@ 159

@@ 161
Each elimination produces a factor on the variable's separator. A bad order makes separators large, so the triangular factor fills with nonzeros. The answer itself is the same in any order.

Q4 (Week 6). Half of the matches are inliers and a minimal sample needs 3 matches. About how many RANSAC iterations give a 99% chance of drawing at least one all-inlier sample?
@@ 167
An all-inlier sample has probability 0.5³ = 0.125. N = log(0.01)/log(1 − 0.125) = 4.605/0.1335 ≈ 34.5, so 35.

Q12 (Weeks 3 and 24). What do variable elimination and Gaussian belief propagation have in common?
@@ 209
Both need a global variable ordering
@@ 210
Both remove variables from a Gaussian factor with a Schur complement; GBP does it inside each message and leaves the graph unchanged
@@ 211
Both create fill-in edges in the factor graph
@@ 212
Both give exact marginal variances on graphs with loops
@@ 213
The factor-to-variable message is a local elimination. Elimination applies it once in a global order and is exact; GBP applies it everywhere repeatedly, with exact means at convergence.
