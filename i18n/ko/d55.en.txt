A one-dimensional example with three positions, \(p_0=0\) held fixed, unit weights, and measurements \(p_1-p_0=1.0\), \(p_2-p_1=1.0\), \(p_2-p_0=2.3\):

The matrix is the Laplacian of the triangle graph with the row and column of the fixed node removed. The 0.3 of disagreement around the loop is shared equally: the three residuals are \(0.1,\ 0.1,\ -0.1\).
@@ 35
In general the optimal positions are a linear function of the right-hand side, and the right-hand side is linear in the rotations. That is what (6.16) and its landmark version (6.31) say, \(\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{t}^{*\mathsf T} & \mathbf{m}^{*\mathsf T}\end{bmatrix}\T=-\operatorname{vec}\!\big(\mathbf{R}^*\tilde{\mathbf{V}}\T\mathbf{L}(W^\tau)^{\dagger}\big)\). The dagger is a pseudo-inverse because the full Laplacian is singular: shifting every position by the same vector changes nothing. Substituting a linear function of \(\mathbf{R}\) into a quadratic cost leaves a cost that is quadratic in \(\mathbf{R}\), so the reduced problem is still \(\min \tr(\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{R}\T\mathbf{R})\) with a new \(\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}\), and Shor's relaxation applies as before.

3. Many landmarks: cost and exactness
@@ 37
A typical map has far more landmarks than poses. Two facts keep the method practical. <span class="src">§6.1.3 · pp. 166–167</span>
@@ 38
<strong>Cost.</strong> With many landmarks the slow step is building \(\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}\). The <strong>Schur complement</strong> trick makes that step linear in the number of landmarks, and the other parts of SE-Sync do not get slower (Figure 6.4(b)).

<strong>Exactness.</strong> More landmarks, and more pose-to-landmark edges, raise the noise level up to which the relaxation stays exact (Figure 6.4(c)).

The book names the trick without showing it. The structural reason is the one you met for bundle adjustment in <a href="#d15">Week 3 · Day 3</a>. Order the positions as poses first, landmarks second, and split the weighted Laplacian into blocks:
@@ 42
An off-diagonal entry of a Laplacian is non-zero only where an edge exists. No measurement joins two landmarks, so \(\mathbf{L}_{mm}\) is <em>diagonal</em>: entry \(k\) is the sum of the weights \(\mu_{ik}\) of the edges at landmark \(k\). Inverting a diagonal matrix is one division per landmark, so the work grows in proportion to the number of landmarks.

Filling the gap: why the unit-vector trick is exact
@@ 50
The reformulation (6.33) replaces each range term by \(\gamma_{ij}\norm{\mathbf{t}_j-\mathbf{t}_i-\tilde r_{ij}\mathbf{b}_{ij}}_2^2\) with a new unknown unit vector \(\mathbf{b}_{ij}\). Claim: for any vector \(\mathbf{v}\) and any \(r\ge 0\),
@@ 51
Proof. Expand, using \(\norm{\mathbf{b}}=1\): \(\norm{\mathbf{v}-r\mathbf{b}}^2=\norm{\mathbf{v}}^2-2r\,\mathbf{v}\T\mathbf{b}+r^2\). Only the middle term depends on \(\mathbf{b}\), and it is smallest when \(\mathbf{v}\T\mathbf{b}\) is largest. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality \(\mathbf{v}\T\mathbf{b}\le\norm{\mathbf{v}}\), with equality when \(\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{v}/\norm{\mathbf{v}}\). Substituting gives \(\norm{\mathbf{v}}^2-2r\norm{\mathbf{v}}+r^2=(\norm{\mathbf{v}}-r)^2\).
@@ 52
Example: \(\mathbf{v}=(3,4)\), \(r=4\). Then \(\norm{\mathbf{v}}=5\), the best \(\mathbf{b}=(0.6,0.8)\), and \(\mathbf{v}-r\mathbf{b}=(0.6,0.8)\) has squared length \(1=(5-4)^2\).
@@ 53
So minimizing (6.33) over the \(\mathbf{b}_{ij}\) first gives back (6.32): the two problems have the same optimal poses. In the widget, the violet segment is \(\tilde r\,\mathbf{b}\) for the best \(\mathbf{b}\) and the thick pink segment is what is left over.
@@ 54
The new problem <em>is</em> a QCQP. The range term is now quadratic in the unknowns \(\mathbf{t}_i,\mathbf{t}_j,\mathbf{b}_{ij}\), and the new constraint \(\mathbf{b}_{ij}\T\mathbf{b}_{ij}=1\) is quadratic too. In words, the solver also estimates the bearing that the sensor did not measure, which turns a range into a range-and-bearing measurement like those of Section 1. Shor's relaxation plus the Riemannian Staircase then gives <strong>CORA</strong>, a certifiable solver for range-aided SLAM. <span class="src">§6.1.4.1 · pp. 168–169</span>
