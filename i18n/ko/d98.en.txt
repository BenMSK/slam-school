Every exteroceptive measurement is a function of a feature <em>expressed in the sensor frame</em>. So every measurement Jacobian is a product: a sensor-specific factor (how the reading changes when the feature moves in the sensor frame) times a geometric factor (how the feature in the sensor frame changes when the state changes). The geometric factor only ever sees the feature <em>relative to</em> the sensor, and that single fact produces the unobservable directions of Day 3.

4. Line features
@@ 44
A 3-D line through two points \(\mathbf{p}^w_1, \mathbf{p}^w_2\) is stored in <strong>Plücker coordinates</strong>, equation (11.44): <span class="src">§11.3.1.2 · pp. 322–323</span>
@@ 45
\(\mathbf{v}_\ell\) is the <strong>direction</strong> of the line. \(\mathbf{n}_\ell\) is its <strong>moment</strong>: a vector normal to the plane that contains the line and the origin. The distance from the origin to the line is \(d_\ell = \norm{\mathbf{n}_\ell}/\norm{\mathbf{v}_\ell}\).
@@ 46 attr
A line passes through two points p1 and p2. Together with the origin O they span a shaded triangle. The direction vector v runs from p1 to p2 along the line. The moment vector n points out of the shaded plane. A perpendicular from O to the line has length d equal to the norm of n divided by the norm of v.
@@ 47

@@ 48

@@ 55
The image of the line is \(\boldsymbol{\ell} = \mathbf{K}\,\mathbf{n}^c_\ell\), equation (11.46), where \(\mathbf{K}\) is built from the intrinsics. Only the moment enters. The reason is geometric: the camera centre and the 3-D line span a plane with normal \(\mathbf{n}^c_\ell\), and the image line is where that plane cuts the image. The direction \(\mathbf{v}^c_\ell\) says where the line lies <em>inside</em> that plane (how far away, at what angle), and one image cannot see that. <span class="src">§11.3.1.2 · p. 323</span>
@@ 56
The measurement (11.47) is the signed distance, in the image, from each detected endpoint \(\mathbf{q}_i = (u_i, v_i, 1)\T\) of the segment to the projected line: \(\mathbf{q}_i\T\boldsymbol{\ell}/\sqrt{\ell_1^2 + \ell_2^2}\). For \(\boldsymbol{\ell} = (3, 4, -10)\) and \(\mathbf{q} = (2, 3, 1)\) it is \((6 + 12 - 10)/5 = 1.6\) pixels. The Jacobian is again a chain, \(\mathbf{H}_x = \frac{\partial \mathbf{z}_\ell}{\partial \boldsymbol{\ell}}\frac{\partial \boldsymbol{\ell}}{\partial \mathbf{x}}\).

5. Plane features
@@ 58
A plane is the set of points with \(\mathbf{n}_\pi\cdot\mathbf{x} = d_\pi\): a unit normal and a distance from the origin. In the sensor frame, equation (11.48) gives <span class="src">§11.3.1.2 · p. 323</span>
@@ 59
The normal rotates; the distance shrinks by however far the sensor has moved along the normal. The book then stores the plane as its <strong>closest point</strong> to the origin, \(\mathbf{p}_\pi = d_\pi\mathbf{n}_\pi\), and the measurement (11.49) is that point seen from the sensor, \(\mathbf{z}_\pi = d^c_\pi\mathbf{n}^c_\pi + \boldsymbol{\eta}^\pi\). <span class="src">§11.3.1.2 · p. 324</span> Three numbers for three degrees of freedom, with no unit-length constraint to maintain.
@@ 60
<strong>Worked example.</strong> A ceiling at height 2 m is \(\mathbf{n}^w = (0, 0, 1)\), \(d^w = 2\). A sensor at \(\mathbf{p}_c = (5, 1, 0.5)\) sees it at \(d^c = 2 - 0.5 = 1.5\) m. Moving the sensor sideways changes nothing: a plane measurement is blind to motion parallel to the plane.

<strong>"\(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\) deletes rows."</strong> It multiplies them by 0 or 1. The vector \(\mathbf{z}_p\) always has three entries; switched-off entries are zero and carry no information.
@@ 75
<strong>"A camera frame constrains velocity."</strong> The velocity and bias columns of \(\mathbf{H}_f\) are zero. Those states are seen only through their effect on later poses.
@@ 76
<strong>"Six Plücker numbers mean six unknowns per line."</strong> A line has four degrees of freedom. Scale and the constraint \(\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{v} = 0\) remove two.
@@ 77
<strong>"The closest-point form works for every plane."</strong> A plane through the origin has \(d_\pi = 0\), so its closest point is the origin itself and the normal is lost.
