A dense semantic map keeps, for every small piece of space, a probability for each class. One segmented image is noisy, so the map fuses many with Bayes' rule. Written in log-odds, that fusion is an addition: each observation adds a fixed vector to the cell it touches.

1. Labels on points and surfels
@@ 12
Segmentation networks can label every point of a LiDAR scan. The labels of one scan are noisy and disagree with the next scan's, so the mapping problem is to integrate them over time. SuMa++ stores a class and a confidence in each surfel. Conflicting labels lower the confidence, and surfels with low confidence are filtered out. <span class="src">§16.3.1 · p. 471</span>
@@ 13
The same mechanism handles moving objects. Deleting every point of a movable class throws away parked cars, which are good references for scan registration in a city. Deleting only the surfels whose stored label conflicts with the current scan worked better. <span class="src">§16.3.1 · p. 472</span>
@@ 14
Labels also feed back into SLAM: registration can match only points of compatible classes (compare ICP in <a href="#d74">Week 13 · Day 2</a>), and place descriptors such as Scan Context (<a href="#d76">Week 13 · Day 4</a>) can be extended with class information for loop closure. <span class="src">§16.3.1 · p. 472</span>

2. Voxels: what is observed and what is estimated
@@ 16
Split the environment into classes \(\mathcal{K} = \{0, \dots, K\}\), where 0 is free space and the others are object classes. A <strong>range-category observation</strong> is, for each sensor ray \(b\), a pair \(z = (r_{t,b}, y_{t,b})\): the range at which the ray stopped and the class of what it hit. An RGB-D camera produces one per pixel by pairing the depth image with a segmentation of the colour image. <span class="src">§16.3.2 · p. 473</span>

5. Storing it: the multi-class octree
@@ 44
A regular grid wastes memory on large empty volumes. An <strong>octree</strong> (<a href="#d46">Week 8 · Day 4</a>) divides space recursively into eight octants; a node has either 8 children or none, and a node without children is a <strong>leaf</strong>. In a <strong>multi-class octree</strong> every node stores a log-odds vector. <span class="src">§16.3.2 · pp. 475–476</span>
@@ 45
<strong>Update.</strong> Cast the ray through the tree, expand each observed leaf down to the finest resolution, and apply (16.24).
@@ 46
<strong>Prune.</strong> If the 8 children of a node are leaves with equal log-odds, delete them and make the parent a leaf. A free block of \(16^3 = 4096\) finest cells collapses to one node. Occupied regions rarely prune, since only their surfaces are observed.
@@ 47
<strong>Clamp.</strong> Noise keeps equal cells from reaching exactly equal values. Limiting each entry of \(\mathbf{h}\) to a fixed interval lets cells settle at the same value and prune, at the price of losing information near certainty.
@@ 48
<strong>Keep few classes.</strong> Cost grows linearly with \(K\) and the chance of pruning falls quickly, so a cell may store only its \(\bar{K}\) most likely classes and lump the rest into "others".
@@ 49
A parent's vector can be the average of its children's, or, conservatively, that of the child most likely to be occupied. Several robots can build one such map by agreeing on their local octrees over one-hop communication (ROAM). <span class="src">§16.3.2 · pp. 476–478</span>

Q1. With classes {free, 1, 2}, a cell has h = [0, 1.1, −0.7]. What is the probability of class 1?
@@ 51
e^0 = 1, e^1.1 = 3.00, e^−0.7 = 0.50. Softmax: 3.00/(1 + 3.00 + 0.50) = 0.67.
@@ 52
Q2. A sensor reports "hit, class 1" with probability 0.6 if the cell is class 1, 0.1 if it is class 2, and 0.02 if it is free. What does this observation add to the class-2 entry of h?
@@ 53
Entry 2 of l(z) is ln[p(z | class 2)/p(z | free)] = ln(0.1/0.02) = ln 5 = 1.61. Even a hit with the other label is evidence that the cell is not free.

Q4. Why does free space compress much better than occupied space in a multi-class octree?
@@ 61
Free cells are not stored at all
@@ 62
Whole free volumes are observed by rays passing through and reach the same clamped value, while objects are seen only at their surface
@@ 63
Occupied cells use more bits per entry
@@ 64
Pruning is only defined for class 0
@@ 65
Pruning needs 8 equal children. Rays cross free volumes everywhere and clamping makes their values equal. The inside of an object is never observed, so its cells differ from the surface cells.

Surfel maps keep a class and a confidence per surfel; conflicting labels lower the confidence.
