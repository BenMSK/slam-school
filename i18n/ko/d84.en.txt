@@ 3
After today you can compute range, velocity and angle from FMCW measurements, set a CFAR threshold, fit an ego-velocity to Doppler readings, and describe how a radar SLAM pipeline finds and verifies loop closures.
@@ 4
An FMCW radar sends chirps and compares each echo with the transmitted signal. The beat frequency gives range, the phase change between chirps gives radial velocity, and the phase change between antennas gives angle. The raw output is a noisy power map with speckle and multipath ghosts, so a filter such as CFAR or k-strongest picks the cells that become points. Odometry comes either from Doppler alone, by fitting the sensor velocity to the radial velocities of static points, or from matching scans as with LiDAR, usually against several keyframes and after motion compensation. Place recognition adapts LiDAR descriptors by storing intensity instead of height. A full system feeds odometry and carefully verified loop constraints into an ordinary pose graph.

An FMCW radar uses chirps with \(B = 750\) MHz and \(T = 50\ \mu\text{s}\). A target produces a beat frequency of 2 MHz. (a) How far away is it? (b) What is the range resolution? (c) With 256 complex samples per chirp, what is the largest unambiguous range? Use \(c = 3\times10^8\) m/s.
@@ 64
(a) \(S = B/T = 7.5\times10^{8}/5\times10^{-5} = 1.5\times10^{13}\) Hz/s. \(d = c f_0/(2S) = (3\times10^{8})(2\times10^{6})/(3\times10^{13}) = 20\) m.
@@ 65
(c) \(d_{\max} = N\,c/(2B) = 256 \times 0.2 = 51.2\) m. Check: the sampling rate is \(N/T = 5.12\) MHz, and a target at 51.2 m has \(f_0 = 2Sd/c = 5.12\) MHz.

A radar has \(\lambda = 3.9\) mm and sends chirps \(T = 60\ \mu\text{s}\) apart. (a) The beat phase changes by 0.8 rad between two chirps. What is the radial velocity? (b) What is \(v_{\max}\)? (c) A target recedes at 20 m/s. What velocity does the radar report?
@@ 67
(c) The true phase change is \(4\pi v T/\lambda = 4\pi(20)(6\times10^{-5})/(3.9\times10^{-3}) = 3.867\) rad, which is more than \(\pi\). The radar sees it wrapped: \(3.867 - 2\pi = -2.416\) rad, and reports \(v = (3.9\times10^{-3})(-2.416)/(7.54\times10^{-4}) = -12.5\) m/s. Equivalently \(20 - 2v_{\max} = -12.5\). The target seems to approach.

A 2D radar reports closing speeds for five points: 8.00 m/s at azimuth 0°, 1.00 m/s at 90°, −1.00 m/s at −90°, 6.36 m/s at 45°, and −2.00 m/s at 180°. (a) Fit the sensor velocity to the first four points by least squares. (b) Compute the residual of the fifth point and interpret it. (c) Fit all five points and compare.
@@ 69
(a) The rows of \(\mathbf{A}\) are \((1,0)\), \((0,1)\), \((0,-1)\), \((0.707, 0.707)\). Then
@@ 70
The determinant is \(1.5 \times 2.5 - 0.25 = 3.5\), so \(v_x = (2.5 \times 12.5 - 0.5 \times 6.5)/3.5 = 8\) and \(v_y = (1.5 \times 6.5 - 0.5 \times 12.5)/3.5 = 1\). The sensor moves at (8, 1) m/s, and all four residuals are zero.
@@ 71
(b) A static point at 180° would read \(8\cos180° + 1\sin180° = -8\) m/s. The measurement is −2, so the residual is \(-2 - (-8) = 6\) m/s. The point is not static: it is something behind the sensor that follows it at 6 m/s along the line of sight.
@@ 72
(c) The fifth row is \((-1, 0)\). Now \(\mathbf{A}\T\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 2.5 & 0.5 \\ 0.5 & 2.5 \end{bmatrix}\) and \(\mathbf{A}\T\mathbf{y} = (12.5 + 2,\ 6.5) = (14.5,\ 6.5)\). The determinant is 6, so \(v_x = (2.5 \times 14.5 - 0.5 \times 6.5)/6 = 5.5\) and \(v_y = (2.5 \times 6.5 - 0.5 \times 14.5)/6 = 1.5\). One moving point out of five has pulled the forward speed from 8 to 5.5 m/s. This is why the outlier is rejected before the fit.
@@ 73
A CA-CFAR filter uses \(N = 8\) training cells and \(P_{fa} = 10^{-4}\). (a) Compute the multiplier \(\alpha\). (b) The eight training cells have powers that sum to 12, and the cell under test has power 30. Is it a detection? (c) One training cell, of power 1.5, is replaced by a second target of power 40. Is the cell under test still detected? (d) Show that \(\alpha \to -\ln P_{fa}\) as \(N \to \infty\), and give its value here.
@@ 74
(b) The mean is \(12/8 = 1.5\), the threshold is \(17.30 \times 1.5 = 25.9\). Since \(30 \gt 25.9\), it is a detection.
@@ 75
(c) The sum becomes \(12 - 1.5 + 40 = 50.5\), the mean 6.31, the threshold \(17.30 \times 6.31 = 109.2\). Now \(30 \lt 109.2\) and the target is missed. A strong neighbour inside the training window masks a weaker target. The median of the eight cells would hardly move, which is the argument for OS-CFAR.
@@ 76
(d) Write \(P_{fa}^{-1/N} = e^{-\ln P_{fa}/N}\). For large \(N\) the exponent is small and \(e^{x} \approx 1 + x\), so \(\alpha = N(e^{-\ln P_{fa}/N} - 1) \approx N \cdot (-\ln P_{fa}/N) = -\ln P_{fa}\). For \(P_{fa} = 10^{-4}\) this is 9.21: the threshold you would use if the noise level were known exactly. With 8 cells the multiplier is almost twice that.
