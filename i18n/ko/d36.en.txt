@@ 3
After today you can choose between RANSAC, pairwise consistency, robust losses, and graduated non-convexity for a given problem, and carry out one step of each by hand.
@@ 4
Least squares assumes Gaussian errors, and one grossly wrong measurement, usually a wrong data association, can move its answer arbitrarily far. Chapter 3 defends in two layers. In the front-end, RANSAC samples minimal sets and keeps the model most measurements agree with, and pairwise consistency maximization keeps the largest group of measurements that agree with each other, found as a maximum clique. In the back-end, M-estimation swaps the square for a robust loss, IRLS solves it as repeated weighted least squares, Black-Rangarajan duality explains the weights, and graduated non-convexity makes IRLS far less dependent on its starting point. None of these comes with a guarantee, and the strongest results combine the layers.

@@ 55

@@ 56

@@ 57

@@ 58

@@ 59
The band is r² ∈ [μ/(μ+1) β², (μ+1)/μ β²]. Both ends tend to β² as μ → ∞, so the surrogate becomes min{r², β²} and the weights become binary.

A 3D-3D registration front-end uses a 3-point minimal solver. (a) With 60% outliers, find the expected number of RANSAC iterations to draw an all-inlier sample, and the number needed for 99% confidence. (b) Repeat for 90% outliers.
@@ 61
(a) \(\omega = 0.4\), \(\omega^3 = 0.064\). Expected: \(1/0.064 = 15.6\). For 99%: \(k \ge \log(0.01)/\log(1-0.064) = (-4.605)/(-0.0661) = 69.6\), so 70 iterations.
@@ 62
(b) \(\omega = 0.1\), \(\omega^3 = 0.001\). Expected: 1000. For 99%: \((-4.605)/\log(0.999) = (-4.605)/(-0.0010005) = 4603\) iterations. Going from 60% to 90% outliers costs a factor of about 65, which is why the chapter turns to PCM for very high outlier rates.

A robot drives down a corridor and back (treat it as one-dimensional). Five candidate loop closures each link an early pose \(a_i\) to a late pose \(b_i\). For each one you compute \(e_i\) = (loop-closure displacement) − (displacement predicted by chaining odometry from \(a_i\) to \(b_i\)), and get \(e = \{0.42,\ 0.38,\ 2.90,\ 0.45,\ -1.70\}\) m. (a) Show that the cycle test (3.6) for loop closures \(i\) and \(j\) reduces to \(|e_i - e_j|\le\gamma\). (b) With \(\gamma = 0.15\) m, build the consistency graph and find the maximum clique. (c) What do the kept loop closures say about the odometry?
@@ 64
(a) In one dimension poses compose by addition and the identity is 0. Go around the cycle \(a_i \to b_i \to b_j \to a_j \to a_i\): loop closure \(i\) forwards (\(T_i\)), odometry from \(b_i\) to \(b_j\), loop closure \(j\) backwards (\(-T_j\)), odometry from \(a_j\) to \(a_i\). Writing \(q\) for positions obtained by chaining odometry, the sum is \(T_i + (q_{b_j}-q_{b_i}) - T_j + (q_{a_i}-q_{a_j}) = [T_i-(q_{b_i}-q_{a_i})] - [T_j-(q_{b_j}-q_{a_j})] = e_i - e_j\).
@@ 65
(b) There are \(5\times4/2 = 10\) pairs. \(|e_1-e_2| = 0.04\), \(|e_1-e_4| = 0.03\), \(|e_2-e_4| = 0.07\): three edges. Every pair involving closure 3 or closure 5 differs by more than 2 m: no edge. The graph is the triangle {1, 2, 4} plus two isolated nodes, so the maximum clique is {1, 2, 4}.
@@ 66
(c) They agree that odometry drifted by about 0.4 m between the two passes. Closures 3 and 5 each imply a different drift that nothing else supports.

(Stretch.) For GNC with the truncated quadratic, the outlier process is \(\Phi_{\rho_\mu}(w) = \dfrac{\mu(1-w)}{\mu+w}\beta^2\) and the weight inside the band is \(w^\star = \beta\sqrt{\mu(\mu+1)}/|r| - \mu\). (a) Substitute \(w^\star\) into \(w\,r^2 + \Phi_{\rho_\mu}(w)\) and show that you get the middle case of (3.24). (b) Evaluate \(w^\star\) and \(\rho_\mu\) for \(\beta = 2,\ \mu = 0.25,\ r = 3\).
@@ 72
(a) Let \(s = \beta\sqrt{\mu(\mu+1)}\), so \(w^\star = s/|r| - \mu\), \(\mu + w^\star = s/|r|\), and \(s^2 = \mu(\mu+1)\beta^2\). First term: \(w^\star r^2 = s|r| - \mu r^2\). Second term: \(\mu\beta^2\,\dfrac{1+\mu-s/|r|}{s/|r|} = \dfrac{\mu(\mu+1)\beta^2\,|r|}{s} - \mu\beta^2 = s|r| - \mu\beta^2\). The sum is \(2s|r| - \mu(\beta^2+r^2) = 2\beta|r|\sqrt{\mu(\mu+1)} - \mu(\beta^2+r^2)\), the middle case of (3.24).
@@ 73
(b) The band is \(r^2\in[\tfrac{0.25}{1.25}\cdot4,\ \tfrac{1.25}{0.25}\cdot4] = [0.8, 20]\), and \(r^2 = 9\) is inside. \(\sqrt{\mu(\mu+1)} = \sqrt{0.3125} = 0.559\). \(w^\star = 2(0.559)/3 - 0.25 = 0.1227\). \(\rho_\mu = 2(2)(3)(0.559) - 0.25(4+9) = 6.708 - 3.25 = 3.458\). Check through the weight: \(0.1227\times9 + 0.25\times4\times\tfrac{0.8773}{0.3727} = 1.104 + 2.354 = 3.458\). The plain truncated quadratic would give weight 0 and loss 4.
