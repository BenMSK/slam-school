The book gives no equations for Gaussian belief propagation, so today supplies them. There are three rules, each using only what one node and its neighbours hold: a variable adds up the messages it receives, tells each factor everything it knows except what that factor told it, and a factor answers by eliminating its other variables. On a tree this is variable elimination done in parallel. On a graph with loops you repeat it, and the means converge to the least-squares solution.

1. What the chapter asks of an algorithm
@@ 10
If computing and storage are spread over many small units (<a href="#d141">Day 3</a>), estimation must live there too. <strong>Belief propagation (BP)</strong> has this character and tolerates arbitrary, asynchronous schedules. <strong>Gaussian belief propagation (GBP)</strong> is BP when every factor and variable is Gaussian, which covers least-squares estimation (<a href="#d8">Week 2 · Day 2</a>). <strong>Rule 3, factor to variable.</strong> To send to \(x_i\), the factor takes its own \((\boldsymbol{\eta}_f, \boldsymbol{\Lambda}_f)\), adds the incoming message from every other variable \(x_j\) to that variable's diagonal block and to its part of \(\boldsymbol{\eta}\), and marginalizes all the \(x_j\) out by the Schur complement. A Gaussian over \(x_i\) alone is left.

In covariance form the variance is \(1/\Lambda_{f\to x_j} = 1/\Lambda_{\text{in}} + 1/w = \sigma_{\text{in}}^2 + \sigma^2\) and the mean is \(\eta/\Lambda = \mu_{\text{in}} + z\). The message says: "\(x_j\) is where \(x_i\) thinks it is, plus the measured step, and the uncertainties add." The other direction gives mean \(\mu_{\text{in}} - z\). If nothing has arrived, \(\Lambda_{\text{in}} = 0\) and the outgoing message is zero too.

5. How this relates to elimination
@@ 39
Rule 3 is one step of variable elimination (<a href="#d14">Week 3 · Day 2</a>): absorb what is known about a factor's other variables, then eliminate them. On a graph without loops, messages sent from the leaves inward are elimination in some order, and messages sent back outward are back-substitution that also delivers every marginal, so the result is exact. Elimination needs a global ordering and creates fill-in. GBP needs neither and never changes the graph, but on a loopy graph it must iterate.
@@ 40
In a loop, a variable's own message comes back round as if it were independent evidence. For Gaussian models this double counting distorts the variances but cancels in the means: Weiss and Freeman proved that whenever GBP converges, its means are exact.

GBP also takes the nonlinear and robust factors of real problems. <span class="src">§18.6 · p. 541</span> Both are local changes to a factor: a nonlinear factor is re-linearized about the current belief means (<a href="#d10">Week 2 · Day 4</a>), and a robust factor shrinks its own \(w\) when its residual is large. The widget uses the Huber weight of <a href="#d34">Week 6 · Day 4</a>: with the residual in standard deviations \(m\), multiply \(w\) by \(\beta/m\) when \(m \gt \beta = 1.5\). Turn on the outlier, a false loop closure claiming \(x_6 - x_2 = 2.5\) when the truth is 0. Least squares, and GBP with it, drags \(x_6\) to 2.66. With robust factors the outlier's weight falls to 0.27 and \(x_6\) settles at 2.22.

6. Learning inside the factor graph
@@ 46
Section 18.7 wants learning that runs alongside probabilistic inference, is mainly <strong>self-supervised</strong>, is <strong>continual</strong> with no divide between training and test time, and is interpretable at the level of modules. <span class="src">§18.7 · p. 543</span> The chapter's answer is <strong>Bayesian machine learning</strong>: estimate a model's parameters by the same laws as any other unknown. In its words, state estimation and model learning differ only in structure and scale. <span class="src">§18.7 · p. 544</span>
@@ 47
A weight is then just another variable. For a model with one weight, each observed input-output pair is a linear measurement of that weight and adds one term to its information pair, exactly as a sensor reading would. Nothing separates training from use.
@@ 48
<strong>GBP Learning</strong> (Nabarro and colleagues) scales this up with a factor graph shaped like a neural network. The parameters are variables shared across all observations; inputs, outputs, and activations are variables copied per observation; nonlinear factors between layers switch softly, as neurons do. Learning is then inference by ordinary GBP; the book reports a version that learned image denoising and classification. <span class="src">§18.7 · pp. 544–545, Figure 18.4</span>

@@ 67
GBP has three rules: belief = sum of incoming messages; variable to factor = belief minus that factor's message; factor to variable = add incoming messages, then Schur-complement away the other variables.
@@ 68
On a tree GBP is elimination plus back-substitution and is exact. With loops it iterates: the means converge to the least-squares solution and the variances are approximate.
@@ 69
GBP Learning treats network weights as variables in the same graph, so learning becomes continual inference.
