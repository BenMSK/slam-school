Week 15 review: event-based SLAM
@@ 3
After today you can predict the events a brightness change produces, turn events into the common representations, run contrast maximization by hand, and evaluate a trajectory with ATE and RPE.
@@ 4
An event camera's pixels work independently and report only changes: when a pixel's log intensity has moved by the contrast threshold \(C\) since its last event, it sends its position, the time, and the sign of the change. That gives microsecond timing, low latency, low power, and a dynamic range above 120 dB, but the data is sparse, asynchronous, noisy, and depends on the motion, because events sample \(\nabla L \cdot \mathbf{v}\). Methods are sorted by how many events they process at once, by whether they are designed or learned, and by whether they extract features (indirect) or use all events (direct). Front-ends often repack events as event frames, time surfaces, or voxel grids. Back-ends are rare and recent; the direct, event-only ones maximize contrast or minimize a per-event brightness error, so far for rotational motion. Simulators, datasets, and the ATE and RPE metrics are how the field measures itself.

Q8. An estimate has 50 steps. Forty-nine relative motions are exact and one has a translation error of 0.7 m. What is the translational RPE (root mean square)?
@@ 67

A pixel with \(C = 0.25\) has just fired while the light on it was 200 units. The light then goes to 600, then to 300, then to 1000. List the events in order, and give the brightness that corresponds to the pixel's final memorized level.
@@ 69
Measure everything from the starting level \(L_0 = \ln 200\). The memory starts at \(L_0\).
@@ 70
To 600: \(L = L_0 + \ln 3 = L_0 + 1.099\). That is 4.39 thresholds above the memory: <strong>4 ON</strong>. Memory is now \(L_0 + 1.0\).
@@ 71
To 300: \(L = L_0 + \ln 1.5 = L_0 + 0.405\), which is \(0.595\) below the memory, 2.38 thresholds: <strong>2 OFF</strong>. Memory is now \(L_0 + 0.5\).
@@ 72
To 1000: \(L = L_0 + \ln 5 = L_0 + 1.609\), which is \(1.109\) above the memory, 4.44 thresholds: <strong>4 ON</strong>. Memory is now \(L_0 + 1.5\).
@@ 73
The final memorized level corresponds to \(200\,e^{1.5} = 896\) units. Ten events in all: 4 ON, 2 OFF, 4 ON.
@@ 74
A pattern with log intensity \(L(x) = 2\sin(2\pi x/40)\), \(x\) in pixels, slides to the right at 400 px/s past a pixel with \(C = 0.2\). (a) Use the linearized model to find the largest event rate. (b) When is the rate zero? (c) How many events does the pixel fire per second on average? Compare with (a).
@@ 75
(a) The steepest slope is \(\max |\partial L/\partial x| = 2 \times 2\pi/40 = 0.314\) per pixel. The rate is \(|\nabla L \cdot v|/C = 0.314 \times 400 / 0.2 = 628\) events per second, at the zero crossings of the sine.
@@ 76
(b) At the crests and troughs, where the slope is zero. The pixel is momentarily looking at a flat piece of the pattern.
@@ 77
(c) One period passes in \(40/400 = 0.1\) s. In that time \(L\) rises by 4 (from \(-2\) to \(2\)) and falls by 4. A rise of 4 is \(4/0.2 = 20\) ON events and the fall is 20 OFF events, so 40 events per 0.1 s, or 400 per second. This is \(2/\pi = 0.64\) of the peak rate, which is the average of \(|\cos|\) over a period, as the linearized model predicts.
@@ 78
Six events come from two edges moving at the same speed: \((x, t) = (5, 0), (7, 1), (9, 2)\) and \((12, 0), (14, 1), (16, 2)\), with \(x\) in pixels and \(t\) in ms. The image has 20 pixels and \(t_\text{ref} = 0\). (a) Compute the variance of the image of warped events for \(\theta = 0, 1, 2, 3\) px/ms. (b) Now try \(\theta = -1.5\), sharing an event that lands halfway between two pixels equally between them. What does the result tell you about the shape of the objective?
@@ 79
The mean is always \(\mu_H = 6/20 = 0.3\), so \(f = \frac{1}{20}\sum_i H_i^2 - 0.09\).
@@ 80
(a) \(\theta = 0\): positions 5, 7, 9, 12, 14, 16; six pixels hold 1; \(f = 6/20 - 0.09 = 0.21\). \(\theta = 1\): positions 5, 6, 7 and 12, 13, 14; again \(f = 0.21\). \(\theta = 2\): positions 5, 5, 5 and 12, 12, 12; two pixels hold 3; \(f = 18/20 - 0.09 = 0.81\). \(\theta = 3\): positions 5, 4, 3 and 12, 11, 10; \(f = 0.21\). The maximum is at \(\theta = 2\) px/ms.
@@ 81
(b) \(x' = x + 1.5\,t\): the first edge goes to 5, 8.5, 12 and the second to 12, 15.5, 19. Pixel 12 receives one event from each edge. The image is 1 at pixel 5, 0.5 at pixels 8 and 9, 2 at pixel 12, 0.5 at pixels 15 and 16, and 1 at pixel 19. \(\sum H_i^2 = 1 + 0.25 + 0.25 + 4 + 0.25 + 0.25 + 1 = 7\), so \(f = 0.35 - 0.09 = 0.26\). That is above the 0.21 of its neighbours although the motion is wrong: events of different edges met by chance. The objective can have small local maxima away from the true motion, which is why the optimizer should start near the answer, for example from the previous estimate.
@@ 82
A robot moves along a line. The true positions at five timestamps are 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 m. An odometry estimate gives 0, 1.1, 2.2, 3.3, 4.4 m. (a) Compute the translational RPE for consecutive poses. (b) Compute the ATE, where "alignment" in one dimension is the best constant shift. (c) What happens to each metric if the run continues in the same way to 17 poses?
@@ 83
(a) Every true step is 1 m and every estimated step is 1.1 m, so each relative error is 0.1 m and the RPE is 0.1 m.
@@ 84
(b) The differences true minus estimate are \(0, -0.1, -0.2, -0.3, -0.4\). The least-squares shift is their mean, \(-0.2\). The residuals are \(0.2, 0.1, 0, -0.1, -0.2\), and
@@ 85
Without the shift (start points matched) it would be \(\sqrt{(0 + 0.01 + 0.04 + 0.09 + 0.16)/5} = 0.245\) m.
@@ 86
(c) The RPE stays 0.1 m. The residuals after the shift are \(0.1\,(i - \bar{\imath})\) for \(i = 0, \dots, n-1\), whose root mean square is \(0.1\sqrt{(n^2 - 1)/12}\). For \(n = 5\) this gives \(0.1\sqrt{2} = 0.141\) as before; for \(n = 17\) it gives \(0.1\sqrt{24} = 0.49\) m. The ATE grows with the length of the run, and the RPE does not. That is the difference between global accuracy and local consistency.
