Week 1 review: the SLAM problem and its toolkit
@@ 3
After today you can describe a SLAM system and its history in a few sentences, fuse Gaussian estimates in information form, and solve a small weighted least-squares problem by hand with a Cholesky factorization.
@@ 4
SLAM estimates a robot's trajectory and a map together. A sensor-specific front-end turns raw data into odometry, landmark observations, and loop closures; a back-end finds the trajectory and map that fit them best. Odometry alone drifts, and loop closures bound the drift. The back-end serves a fast control loop and a slow planning loop and must stay online. Its history runs from the EKF through particle filters to factor-graph optimization, which won because the information matrix is sparse. The math underneath is short: beliefs are Gaussians, Bayes' rule combines them, independent information adds, and the MAP estimate under Gaussian noise is a weighted least-squares problem, solved by factoring a matrix and never by inverting it.

@@ 74
(a) Bayes' rule: \(\dfrac{0.9\times 0.02}{0.9\times 0.02 + 0.05\times 0.98} = \dfrac{0.018}{0.067} \approx 0.27\). Still more likely a look-alike than a revisit.
@@ 75
(b) The posterior from (a) becomes the new prior: \(\dfrac{0.9\times 0.269}{0.9\times 0.269 + 0.05\times 0.731} = \dfrac{0.242}{0.278} \approx 0.87\). Independent evidence compounds: two agreeing checks are worth far more than one.
