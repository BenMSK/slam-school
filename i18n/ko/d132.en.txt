@@ 4
A metric-semantic map stores what things are as well as where they are. Sparse maps turn landmarks into objects with a class, pose, scale, and shape, and compare each shape model with the matching detector output: keypoints, boxes, masks, or depth. Classes and data associations are discrete, so the back-end alternates between choosing them (hard, or softly with EM) and solving least squares. Dense maps keep a class distribution per voxel and fuse labels by adding log-odds. Meshes follow a loop closure through a deformation graph, and scene graphs stack objects, places, and rooms on top, storing each symbol once.

@@ 53
A robot moves along a line. The map has two chairs, A at 2.0 m and B at 3.0 m. The robot believes it is at \(p = 0.4\) m and receives two detections of objects ahead, \(z_1 = 1.9\) m and \(z_2 = 2.7\) m, with \(\sigma = 0.5\) m and no useful labels. Do one EM iteration for \(p\). Compare with a hard nearest-neighbour step.
@@ 54
E-step. The detections are predicted at \(2.3\) and \(3.1\) m. With \(e^{-d^2/2\sigma^2} = e^{-2d^2}\): for \(z_1\), distances \(0.3\) and \(0.7\) give \(0.835\) and \(0.375\), so \(w_{1A} = 0.69\), \(w_{1B} = 0.31\). For \(z_2\), distances \(1.1\) and \(0.1\) give \(0.089\) and \(0.980\), so \(w_{2A} = 0.08\), \(w_{2B} = 0.92\).
@@ 55
M-step. Minimizing \(\sum_{k,j} w_{kj}(z_k + p - \ell_j)^2\) gives the weighted mean of the values \(\ell_j - z_k\), with weights summing to 2: \(p = \tfrac12[0.69(0.1) + 0.31(1.1) + 0.08(-0.7) + 0.92(0.3)] = 0.31\) m.
@@ 56
Nearest neighbour assigns \(z_1\) to A and \(z_2\) to B: \(p = \tfrac12(0.1 + 0.3) = 0.2\) m. EM differs because it still gives 31% to the first detection being chair B.
@@ 57
A voxel has classes {free, wall, door} and starts with all three equally likely. The sensor reports "hit, wall" with probability 0.04, 0.64, 0.16 when the cell is free, wall, door; "hit, door" with 0.04, 0.16, 0.64; and passes through with 0.92, 0.2, 0.2. The cell receives: hit wall, hit wall, hit door, pass. Find the class probabilities.
@@ 58
Log-odds increments relative to "free": \(\mathbf{l}(\text{hit wall}) = [0, \ln 16, \ln 4]\), \(\mathbf{l}(\text{hit door}) = [0, \ln 4, \ln 16]\), \(\mathbf{l}(\text{pass}) = [0, \ln 0.217, \ln 0.217]\). Adding logs is multiplying ratios: wall \(16 \cdot 16 \cdot 4 \cdot 0.217 = 222.6\), door \(4 \cdot 4 \cdot 16 \cdot 0.217 = 55.7\), free \(1\). So \(\mathbf{h} = [0, 5.41, 4.02]\) and \(p = [1, 222.6, 55.7]/279.3 = [0.004, 0.797, 0.199]\). Wall leads door four to one: a net of one label vote, worth \(0.64/0.16\).

Mesh control nodes \(k\) and \(l\) start at \(\mathbf{g}_k = (0,0,0)\) and \(\mathbf{g}_l = (2,0,0)\). After a loop closure both have rotated by 90° about the \(z\)-axis; \(\mathbf{t}_k = (0,0,0)\) and \(\mathbf{t}_l = (0, 2, 0.3)\). (a) Compute the rigidity residual of edge \((k, l)\) in (16.27) and its cost for \(\omega_t = 4\). (b) A third node \(j\) at \(\mathbf{g}_j = (1, 2, 0)\) did not move. Where does the mesh vertex \(\mathbf{v} = (1, 0, 0)\) go under (16.32) with \(m = 3\)?
@@ 60
(a) A 90° rotation about \(z\) maps \((x, y, z)\) to \((-y, x, z)\). So \(\mathbf{R}_k(\mathbf{g}_l - \mathbf{g}_k) + \mathbf{t}_k - \mathbf{t}_l = (0, 2, 0) + (0,0,0) - (0, 2, 0.3) = (0, 0, -0.3)\). Cost: \(4 \times 0.09 = 0.36\).
@@ 61
(b) Distances from \(\mathbf{v}\): 1 to \(k\), 1 to \(l\), 2 to \(j\), so \(d_{\max} = 2\), \(\bar{w} = (0.25, 0.25, 0)\), and \(w = (0.5, 0.5, 0)\). Node \(k\) sends \(\mathbf{v}\) to \(\mathbf{R}_k(1,0,0) + \mathbf{t}_k = (0, 1, 0)\). Node \(l\) sends it to \(\mathbf{R}_l(-1, 0, 0) + \mathbf{t}_l = (0, -1, 0) + (0, 2, 0.3) = (0, 1, 0.3)\). The blend is \(\tilde{\mathbf{v}} = (0, 1, 0.15)\).
