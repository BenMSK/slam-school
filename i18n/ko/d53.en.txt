3. Theorem 6.2 and the Riemannian staircase
@@ 40
There is an obvious objection. Problem 6.4 is non-convex, so its local solver can get stuck, and we seem to be back where we started. The answer is this result, adapted from Boumal and co-authors: <span class="src">Theorem 6.2 · p. 163</span>
@@ 41
<em>Suppose \(\mathbf{Y}\in\operatorname{St}(d,r)^n\) is a second-order critical point of Problem 6.4 and is (row) rank-deficient. Then \(\mathbf{Y}\) is a global minimizer of Problem 6.4, and \(\mathbf{Z}^\star=\mathbf{Y}\T\mathbf{Y}\) solves the SDP relaxation, Problem 6.3.</em>
@@ 42
The terms: a <strong>first-order critical point</strong> has zero Riemannian gradient. A <strong>second-order critical point</strong> in addition has no direction of negative curvature within the tangent space; this is what a good second-order local solver converges to. <strong>Row rank-deficient</strong> means \(\rank(\mathbf{Y})\lt r\): the \(r\) rows of \(\mathbf{Y}\) are linearly dependent, so the solution fits in fewer dimensions than it was offered.

Filling the gap: why a spare dimension proves optimality (sketch)
@@ 44
The book cites the theorem without proof. The argument is short once Day 4 is in place.
@@ 45
First-order critical means \(\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{0}\) (see the box above): condition (i).
@@ 46
For this cost, the second derivative along a tangent direction \(\mathbf{V}\) at a critical point is \(2\tr(\mathbf{V}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{V}\T)\). (We take this formula from the references.) Second-order critical means it is non-negative for every tangent \(\mathbf{V}\).
@@ 47
Rank-deficient means there is a unit vector \(\mathbf{u}\in\R^r\) perpendicular to every column of \(\mathbf{Y}\): \(\mathbf{u}\T\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{0}\). Take any \(\mathbf{v}\in\R^{dn}\) and set \(\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}\T\). It is tangent, because \(\mathbf{Y}_i\T\mathbf{V}_i=(\mathbf{Y}_i\T\mathbf{u})\mathbf{v}_i\T=\mathbf{0}\). It moves the poses into the unused dimension \(\mathbf{u}\).
@@ 48
Insert it into step 2: \(2\tr(\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}\T\mathbf{S}\mathbf{v}\mathbf{u}\T)=2(\mathbf{v}\T\mathbf{S}\mathbf{v})(\mathbf{u}\T\mathbf{u})=2\,\mathbf{v}\T\mathbf{S}\mathbf{v}\ge0\). This holds for every \(\mathbf{v}\), so \(\mathbf{S}\succeq0\): condition (ii).
@@ 49
Conditions (i) and (ii) are the certificate of Day 4, so \(\mathbf{Y}\T\mathbf{Y}\) solves the SDP.
@@ 50
In words: an unused dimension is an escape hatch. If any feasible \(\mathbf{Z}\) had a lower cost, there would be a way down through the hatch, and a second-order critical point has no way down.

4. Rounding: from Y back to rotations
@@ 68
The staircase returns \(\mathbf{Y}^\star\in\operatorname{St}(d,r)^n\), which has \(r\) rows. The answer we want has \(d\) rows and blocks in \(\SO(d)\). If the relaxation is exact, then <span class="src">eq. (6.25) · p. 163</span>
@@ 69
so \(\mathbf{Y}^\star\) has rank \(d\) and \(\mathbf{R}^\star\) can be read off from a thin singular value decomposition (<a href="#d5">Week 1 · Day 5</a>). Write \(\mathbf{Y}^\star=\mathbf{U}_d\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_d\mathbf{V}_d\T\), keeping the \(d\) nonzero singular values. Then \(\hat{\mathbf{R}}=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_d\mathbf{V}_d\T\) is \(d\times dn\) and satisfies \(\hat{\mathbf{R}}\T\hat{\mathbf{R}}=\mathbf{Y}^{\star\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{Y}^\star\). Two \(d\times dn\) matrices with the same product \(\mathbf{R}\T\mathbf{R}\) differ only by an orthogonal matrix on the left, so \(\hat{\mathbf{R}}=\mathbf{G}\mathbf{R}^\star\): the optimal rotations up to one global rotation (and, if the blocks come out with determinant \(-1\), one reflection to undo). That global rotation is the gauge freedom, which no relative measurement can fix.

If the relaxation is not exact, \(\mathbf{Y}^\star\) has rank above \(d\). Then keep its \(d\) largest singular values (the best rank-\(d\) approximation), and replace each \(d\times d\) block of the result by the nearest rotation, again with an SVD. The outcome is feasible but only approximately optimal. <span class="src">§6.1.2.4 · pp. 163–164</span> All of this works on the small factor \(\mathbf{Y}^\star\); the big matrix \(\mathbf{Z}^\star\) is never formed.
