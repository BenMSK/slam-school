Week 4 review: Lie groups and optimization on manifolds
@@ 3
After today you can move between a pose and its tangent vector, run one Gauss-Newton step on a group by hand, state what a covariance of a pose means, and use the adjoint to change the side of a perturbation.
@@ 4
Rotations and poses are matrices tied by constraints, so they form curved manifolds, \(\SO(d)\) and \(\SE(d)\), not vector spaces. They are also groups, and each has a flat companion, its Lie algebra, with one coordinate per degree of freedom. The exponential map turns a vector of the Lie algebra into a group member, and the logarithm goes back. That is enough to rebuild everything Week 2 did with vectors: a perturbation \(\mathbf{T}^0\,\Exp(\boldsymbol{\xi})\) replaces "guess plus step" in Gauss-Newton, \(\bar{\mathbf{T}}\,\Exp(\boldsymbol{\delta})\) with Gaussian \(\boldsymbol{\delta}\) replaces "mean plus noise", and \(\Log\) of a relative transformation replaces subtraction. Because order matters in a group, two more tools are needed: the adjoint, which moves a perturbation across a transformation exactly, and the Jacobian, which combines two exponentials approximately.

Q1. How many degrees of freedom does a planar pose in \(\SE(2)\) have?
@@ 27
Two for translation and one for the heading: \(3(d-1)\) with \(d=2\). The 3 × 3 matrix has nine entries, but most are fixed.
@@ 28
Q2. You know \(\mathbf{T}^b_a\) and \(\mathbf{T}^c_b\). Which product converts coordinates from frame \(a\) to frame \(c\)?
@@ 29
The matrix nearest the point acts first, and adjacent indices must match: \(\mathbf{T}^c_a = \mathbf{T}^c_b\mathbf{T}^b_a\). The other order does not have matching indices, and poses do not commute.

@@ 32

Q7. One-step model of Day 4 with \(L = 10\) m and heading noise \(\sigma = 0.1\) rad. What is the standard deviation of the sideways tangent coordinate \(\rho_2\)?
@@ 48
The covariance is \(\sigma^2(0,L,1)\T(0,L,1)\). Its middle diagonal entry is \(\sigma^2L^2 = 1\ \text{m}^2\), so the standard deviation is \(L\sigma = 1\) m.

Gauss-Newton on \(\SO(2)\), by hand. A robot with unknown heading \(\mathbf{R}\in\SO(2)\) observes one known landmark \(\boldsymbol{\ell} = (1,0)\) and measures \(\mathbf{z} = \mathbf{R}\boldsymbol{\ell} + \boldsymbol{\eta} = (0.6, 0.8)\), with \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma} = \mathbf{I}\). Start from \(\mathbf{R}^0 = \mathbf{I}\) and perturb on the right, \(\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{R}^0\Exp(\theta)\). (a) Find the Jacobian of \(\mathbf{R}\boldsymbol{\ell}\) with respect to \(\theta\). (b) Do two Gauss-Newton iterations. (c) Compare with the exact answer.
@@ 64
(a) To first order \(\mathbf{R}^0\Exp(\theta)\boldsymbol{\ell}\approx\mathbf{R}^0(\mathbf{I}+\theta\mathbf{S})\boldsymbol{\ell} = \mathbf{R}^0\boldsymbol{\ell} + \mathbf{R}^0\mathbf{S}\boldsymbol{\ell}\,\theta\). So \(\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{R}^0\mathbf{S}\boldsymbol{\ell}\), a 2 × 1 matrix, with \(\mathbf{S}\boldsymbol{\ell} = (0,1)\). It is a unit vector for every \(\mathbf{R}^0\), so \(\mathbf{H}\T\mathbf{H} = 1\) and the normal equations reduce to \(\theta^* = \mathbf{H}\T\mathbf{r}\).
@@ 65
(b) <strong>Iteration 1.</strong> \(\mathbf{R}^0 = \mathbf{I}\): prediction \((1,0)\), residual \(\mathbf{r} = (0.6,0.8)-(1,0) = (-0.4, 0.8)\), \(\mathbf{H} = (0,1)\), so \(\theta^* = 0.8\). Update: \(\mathbf{R}^0\leftarrow\Exp(0.8)\), a rotation by 0.8 rad.
@@ 66
<strong>Iteration 2.</strong> Prediction \(\mathbf{R}^0\boldsymbol{\ell} = (\cos 0.8, \sin 0.8) = (0.6967, 0.7174)\). Residual \(\mathbf{r} = (-0.0967, 0.0826)\). \(\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{R}^0(0,1) = (-0.7174, 0.6967)\). Then \(\theta^* = (-0.7174)(-0.0967) + (0.6967)(0.0826) = 0.0694 + 0.0576 = 0.1269\). Update: \(\mathbf{R}^0\leftarrow\mathbf{R}^0\Exp(0.1269)\), a rotation by \(0.9269\) rad in total.
@@ 67
(c) The measurement is a unit vector, so the exact answer is the heading with \(\cos\theta = 0.6\), \(\sin\theta = 0.8\): \(\theta = 0.9273\) rad (53.13°). Two iterations are within 0.0004 rad. At every iteration the estimate was a rotation matrix. An additive update of the four matrix entries would have left the circle after the first step.
