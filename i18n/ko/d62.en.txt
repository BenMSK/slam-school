A camera records directions to things, not distances. Depth appears only when the same point is seen from two places, and its accuracy depends on how far apart those places are. Everything in visual SLAM, from the 19th-century surveyors to a headset tracking in real time, is a way of organizing that triangulation over many points and many views.

2. Five overlapping terms
@@ 41
The book notes that these words are often used interchangeably. They describe similar computations with different emphasis. <span class="src">§7.1.2 · pp. 194–195</span>
@@ 42
What sets it apart
@@ 43
<strong>Photogrammetry</strong>
@@ 44
The science of taking measurements and 3D reconstructions from overlapping photographs
@@ 45
The oldest and broadest term; camera positions are often assumed known
@@ 46
<strong>Bundle adjustment (BA)</strong>
@@ 47
An optimization: adjust camera poses (optionally intrinsics) and 3D points to minimize reprojection error
@@ 48
It is a solver step, not a system. It needs a good initial guess and outlier rejection
@@ 49
<strong>Structure from motion (SfM)</strong>
@@ 50
Estimate the 3D structure and the camera motion together from a collection of images
@@ 51
Usually offline and non-causal; images may be unordered; ends with BA
@@ 52
<strong>Visual odometry (VO)</strong>
@@ 53
Estimate camera motion from consecutive video frames
@@ 54
Local: a sliding window of recent frames, no global map, so it drifts
@@ 55
<strong>Visual SLAM</strong>
@@ 56
Localize and map in real time in an unknown environment
@@ 57
Adds place recognition, relocalization, and loop closure to VO
@@ 58
The <strong>reprojection error</strong> in the BA row is the distance in the image between where a point was observed and where the current 3D model says it should appear. You will write it as a formula on Day 5. BA minimizes it with the Gauss-Newton or Levenberg-Marquardt methods of <a href="#d10">Week 2 · Day 4</a> and <a href="#d11">Week 2 · Day 5</a>.

3. Why vision is harder than LiDAR: depth is not measured
@@ 60
A LiDAR returns the distance to each point. A camera returns the projection of the scene's light onto its sensor, and the 3D geometry has to be inferred. That is why the book calls the visual problem more challenging. <span class="src">§7.2 · p. 196</span>

@@ 65
If each column is measured with independent noise of standard deviation \(\sigma\), then \(\sigma_d = \sqrt{2}\,\sigma\). Numbers: \(f = 500\) px, \(b = 0.5\) m, \(z = 20\) m gives \(d = 12.5\) px. With \(\sigma = 1\) px, \(\sigma_z \approx \frac{400}{250}\times 1.41 = 2.26\) m, an 11% error from one pixel of noise.
@@ 66
Two consequences shape every visual SLAM system. Depth error grows with the <em>square</em> of depth. And it shrinks in proportion to the baseline, so a camera must move sideways enough before a new point can be placed.
@@ 67
Try it: double the baseline, then double the depth.
@@ 68
Notice that the cloud of triangulated points is a thin needle along the viewing direction: sideways position is known well, depth poorly. At the defaults the predicted depth σ is 2.26 m. A baseline of 1 m halves it; a depth of 10 m divides it by four. At the far-left baseline with depth 30 m the disparity is under 2 px, the cloud smears over tens of meters, and some samples give no usable depth at all (the count of valid samples drops below 300).
@@ 69
A stereo rig has a fixed, known baseline. A single moving camera has to use its own motion as the baseline, and that motion is itself unknown. This is the root of two issues you will meet again: a monocular system needs a special initialization step (Day 5), and it cannot recover the absolute scale of the scene.

@@ 71
Like every SLAM system since <a href="#d1">Week 1 · Day 1</a>, a visual one is split into stages. The book names three sub-functions that complement each other. <span class="src">§7.2 · p. 196</span>

The front-end must keep up with the camera. The back-end and place recognition run in parallel at a slower rate and share intermediate results such as keypoints and poses (§7.2.4).
@@ 86
The <strong>visual odometry front-end</strong> estimates the relative motion between consecutive frames and so provides the initial estimate of the camera pose. <span class="src">§7.2.1 · p. 196</span>
@@ 87
The <strong>mapping back-end</strong> refines trajectory and map with global optimization, BA or pose-graph optimization, and folds in the constraints found by place recognition. The result is long-term consistency. <span class="src">§7.2.2 · pp. 196–197</span>
@@ 88
<strong>Visual place recognition and relocalization</strong> matches the current image against earlier ones, with classical descriptors or trained networks. It detects revisits, which corrects drift, and it finds the camera again when tracking is lost. <span class="src">§7.2.3 · p. 197</span>
@@ 89
These run as parallel threads. Heavy jobs like global optimization are scheduled so that tracking stays real-time. <span class="src">§7.2.4 · p. 197</span>

@@ 107
The <strong>basin of convergence</strong> is the set of starting guesses from which the optimizer reaches the right answer. Matched features can pull a poor initial pose a long way, because the match itself says where the point went. Raw brightness only gives a useful gradient within a pixel or two of the right place. Direct methods therefore work <strong>coarse-to-fine</strong>: align heavily down-sampled images first, where a large motion is a small number of pixels, then refine at higher resolution. Some align learned feature maps instead of brightness. <span class="src">§7.2.1 · p. 196</span> Week 12 treats both alignments in detail.
@@ 108
<strong>"Bundle adjustment is a kind of SLAM system."</strong> BA is the optimization problem at the core. SfM and visual SLAM are systems that call it.
@@ 109
<strong>"Visual odometry plus a map is visual SLAM."</strong> The defining addition is recognizing previously visited places and correcting the trajectory around the loop.
@@ 110
<strong>"More pixels of resolution fix depth accuracy."</strong> Depth error scales with \(z^2/(f\,b)\). Without baseline there is no depth, however sharp the image.
